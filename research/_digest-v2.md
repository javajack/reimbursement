# V2 RESEARCH DIGEST

## PART A — Domain research


### [pain-points-software]
Across review sites, app stores, vendor KBs, and bug trackers, the same software-only failures recur in 2023-2026 and none of the seven incumbents has fully solved them. The most damaging cluster is capture+OCR: receipts photographed blurry/wrinkled fail silently or — worse — produce confidently wrong data (an open 2024 Expensify bug shows blurry receipts yielding a 'random amount' with no warning flag), SmartScan-style async OCR officially takes 'a few minutes to a few hours', and Concur shipped a months-long iPhone 15 blurry-camera regression that users worked around by switching phones; no major incumbent ships document-scanner-grade edge-detection capture in its core flow (Emburse/Payhawk market it as a differentiator). Second is the approval black hole: manager inboxes are the admitted #1 bottleneck (Fyle's own blog), rejections often arrive without reasons (Happay G2), rejected reports vanish from AP visibility (Concur forum), and whole-report resubmission punishes employees for single-line errors. Third is reimbursement opacity and speed — a 2024 Webexpenses survey found 43% of UK employees say slow reimbursement hurts their work experience, Indian vendor data claims 8+ day manual cycles, and Happay Play Store reviewers report money 'withdrawn but not disbursed' — confirming 'when do I get paid?' is unanswered even inside the apps. Fourth, pricing models are actively resented: per-user fees plus scan metering (Expensify's 25 free SmartScans/month, $0.20 overage) and Trustpilot-documented overbilling/cancellation traps validate a submissions-metered, unlimited-user model with a soft paywall. India-specific software gaps are concrete: ITC-grade GST capture (employer GSTIN on invoice, CGST/SGST/IGST split) is rarely automated — one vendor pegs up to 60% of ITC on travel spend lost — and Zoho's autoscan language list does not include Hindi or any Indic language. Finance-admin pains (no search-by-amount in Expensify, missing MIS in Happay, no clean Tally JV export, GL-mapping failures in Zoho) and mobile reliability (Happay OTP login failures, Concur upload 'server errors', lost offline drafts) round out the attack surface. GBTA's widely cited baseline ($58 to process a report, 19% error rate, $52+18 minutes per fix) quantifies the cost of every one of these failure loops. Net: a software-only product wins by making capture deterministic (edge-detected, de-skewed, instant-feedback), OCR honest (confidence flags + graceful manual line-item fallback), approvals escalation-driven with per-line rejection, payout status explicit, and pricing per-submission — each of which maps to a documented, unresolved complaint.

**Recommendations:**
- Make capture deterministic, not coached: live edge detection, auto-capture, de-skew, glare/blur detection with immediate retake prompt in the PWA camera flow — every incumbent failure here (Concur iPhone 15 blur, Zoho wrinkled-receipt fails) is a cheap demo-able win
- Engineer OCR for honesty over magic: per-field confidence with visible flags, hard rule that low-confidence never auto-fills amounts (the exact bug Expensify shipped), and one-tap fallback to manual line items at submission — position 'never silently wrong' as the trust claim
- Ship approvals with escalation and per-line rejection ON by default: N-day auto-escalate up the chain, mandatory rejection reason, reject single lines not whole reports, and a shared status view so finance can always see rejected items
- Answer 'when do I get paid?' structurally: employee-visible timeline with expected payout date driven by the bank-file export event; this is the cheapest retention feature in the category and incumbents demonstrably lack it
- Build GST-grade capture as the India moat: GSTIN field extraction + format validation, CGST/SGST/IGST split, flag invoices missing employer GSTIN as 'ITC at risk' for finance, and train/handle Hindi+major Indic-language receipts that Zoho's OCR explicitly cannot read
- Lead pricing with the anti-Expensify story: unlimited users/approvers free, metered submissions, soft paywall that queues instead of blocks, no annual lock-in tricks — Trustpilot billing complaints are ready-made sales ammunition
- Treat Tally JV export + search-by-amount + MIS (processed/failed/pending counts) as launch-blocking finance features, not roadmap items — they are the buyer-persona complaints at Expensify, Happay, and Zoho
- Make email-in receipts loud-failing: verified senders with explicit bounce-back reasons, cross-channel dedupe, and a visible 'received but unprocessed' queue so nothing is silently dropped

**UX patterns:**
- Async OCR queue with 'Scanning...' placeholder (Expensify SmartScan): fails because waits run minutes-to-hours and failures surface as unflagged wrong values; Expensify's own spec in GitHub #45904 (red-dot indicator + 0.00 amount on low confidence) is the right pattern but unimplemented — ship per-field confidence flags and a guaranteed-time or instant fallback instead
- Scan-failed triage bucket (Zoho 'Scan Failed Receipts' list, tap to add manually or delete): works as a graceful soft-fail and directly validates the planned manual-line-item fallback at submission
- Capture coaching via help text ('flat, dark surface, hold steady' — Expensify): anti-pattern that exports the problem to the user; the proven fix is live edge detection + auto-capture + de-skew (Evernote Scannable auto-snaps; Emburse 'smart border cropping' with manual border adjust; Payhawk AI camera) — none of the big-7 core apps do this, which is the capture wedge
- Multiple receipts in one photo silently breaks OCR (Zoho KB): camera flow should detect and split or warn at capture time, not fail downstream
- Email-in receipts with silent drop on unverified sender (Expensify): always bounce with a reason; Expensify also created broken expenses from its own failure notifications (GitHub #71911) — dedupe by message-ID and never ingest own system mail
- Whole-report bounce on rejection (Concur/most): per-line rejection with reason codes (Declaree pattern) keeps the rest of the report moving and kills the $52/18-minute correction loop
- Approval reminders as admin-configured emails (Concur): default-on persistent reminders + auto-escalation to next approver after N days (ExpensePoint/Oracle pattern) beats reminder spam; Fyle reviews show undifferentiated notification volume trains users to ignore the tool
- Rejected reports vanishing from AP view until resubmission (Concur): keep a shared, immutable status timeline visible to employee, approver, and finance simultaneously
- No payout ETA anywhere (category-wide): show a status timeline 'submitted -> approved -> in payout batch -> paid' with expected pay date (Spendesk markets exactly this); with bank bulk-file export, surface 'included in file generated on <date>' as the paid-proxy event
- Forced hotel itemization with rigid math (Concur): if line totals must equal header total, auto-distribute residuals and allow per-night rate variance in one expense — or extract line items from the folio via OCR so the employee only confirms
- Scan-credit hard paywall mid-month (Expensify 25 free scans 'exhausted within 2 weeks', $0.20 overage; Zoho autoscan silently removed from free plan): validates the planned soft paywall — accept and queue uploads, pause intelligent processing, let employees nudge admins, never block submission
- Duplicate detection keyed to a single channel (Fyle card-lag duplicates): fingerprint receipts across camera/email/manual (merchant+date+amount+image hash) with a 'merge' suggestion instead of hard block or silent double-entry

### [mobile-capture-tech]
A document-scanner-grade receipt capture flow is buildable as pure mobile-web in 2026, but only if engineered around a sharp platform split: Android Chrome exposes the full camera toolkit (ImageCapture.takePhoto at full sensor resolution, focusMode/focusDistance, zoom, torch via applyConstraints), while iOS Safari supports none of ImageCapture (not even in Safari 26.5), forces canvas-grab capture capped at the video stream resolution, historically ignores torch (WebKit bug 243075), and re-prompts camera permission in installed PWAs on hash navigation (open WebKit bug 215884). The notorious "stretched receipt" bug has one root cause expressed three ways: the browser delivers a stream whose real aspect ratio (track.getSettings()/video.videoWidth-videoHeight) differs from what was requested, and developers then (a) stretch the preview with CSS object-fit:fill, (b) drawImage onto a canvas with mismatched aspect, or (c) size the canvas from CSS pixels instead of stream pixels; the canonical fix is object-fit:cover for the viewfinder plus a capture canvas sized exactly to videoWidth/videoHeight (MDN pattern), with cover-crop math if the capture must match the visible viewfinder region. In-browser edge detection + perspective correction is a build-vs-buy fork: free jscanify (MIT) rides on an ~8 MB OpenCV.js WASM that takes seconds to initialize and whose threaded build crashes 3 GB-RAM phones, and it requires solid-background contrast; commercial Dynamsoft (from $1,249/yr) and Scanbot (quote-only flat annual fee, unlimited scans) add auto-capture, manual corner editors, and quality scoring. Given India's installed base (Android 92-95%, entry-level median 4 GB RAM), the pragmatic small-team pipeline is: default to <input type=file capture> (native camera app does focus/HDR/orientation for free, works in every in-app browser), progressively enhance to a getUserMedia viewfinder with ImageCapture on Android, run perspective correction server-side (or lazy-load a slim OpenCV.js only on the scan screen), client-compress to ~1600-2048 px long edge JPEG q≈0.8 (~200-500 KB/page, comfortably above Google Vision's 1024x768 OCR recommendation and Tesseract's 300-DPI guidance), and queue uploads in IndexedDB with Workbox Background Sync on Chromium plus online/visibilitychange replay on iOS, since Safari still ships no Background Sync. EXIF orientation is only a risk on the file-input path and is neutralized by re-encoding through canvas (Chrome 81+/Safari honor EXIF in drawImage; re-encode strips the tag). iOS canvas limits (16.78 MP area pre-iOS 18, 67.1 MP from iOS 18) and one-active-stream behavior dictate processing pages one at a time at ≤12 MP. Incumbent expense products (Expensify, Wave, Dynamics 365) keep scanning in native apps and offer only plain upload on web — meaning a polished PWA scanner is a genuine differentiator, but the platform ceilings above are why nobody has made it effortless.

**Recommendations:**
- Ship the file-input path first and keep it forever as the guaranteed fallback: <input type=file accept='image/*' capture='environment'> -> createImageBitmap -> canvas re-encode (EXIF-safe) -> browser-image-compression to 2048 px long edge JPEG q0.8 -> IndexedDB queue -> upload. This alone is competitive with incumbent web flows (Expensify/Wave do less).
- Build the PWA viewfinder as progressive enhancement, Android-first: getUserMedia({video:{facingMode:{ideal:'environment'}, width:{ideal:4096}, height:{ideal:2160}}}), object-fit:cover preview, capture via ImageCapture.takePhoto() when 'ImageCapture' in window, else canvas sized to videoWidth/videoHeight (or the GoogleChromeLabs polyfill to unify the API); read track.getSettings() before drawing any ratio-dependent overlay.
- Never derive capture canvas dimensions from element CSS size or devicePixelRatio; the only authoritative dimensions are video.videoWidth/videoHeight after loadedmetadata/canplay (MDN pattern). Add a one-line assert in dev builds that capture aspect == settings aspect to make distortion regressions impossible.
- Do perspective correction server-side for v1 (OpenCV Python on the uploaded full-res JPEG): keeps 8 MB WASM and multi-second init off 4 GB-RAM Indian Android devices, centralizes quality, and the client still ships a manual 4-corner crop editor as rescue UI. Move detection client-side later only if capture-quality telemetry demands it.
- If/when client-side live detection is added: lazy-load a custom single-threaded SIMD OpenCV.js build only on the scan screen, cache it with the service worker, run detection on a ~720p downscaled frame at <=10 fps, call .delete() on every Mat, and warp the full-res frame only once at capture; never load the threaded build (3 GB-RAM crash, opencv#24986).
- Defer commercial SDKs until receipt-quality complaints have a measured cost: Dynamsoft DDN from $1,249/yr or Scanbot (quote-only, unlimited scans per domain) buy auto-capture + corner editor + DQA out of the box — a sane upgrade at scale, not at MVP.
- Cap processing at 12 MP per page client-side (4032x3024 fits the pre-iOS-18 16.78 MP canvas area); process pages strictly one at a time and release references between pages to respect iOS canvas/memory budgets.
- Offline queue design: IndexedDB records {blob, submissionId, pageIndex, idempotencyKey, attempts, lastError}; upload with fetch + exponential backoff; Workbox BackgroundSyncPlugin where supported; on iOS replay on 'online', visibilitychange, and app launch; rely on Safari 17's 60%-of-disk quota but surface a warning if storage.estimate() shows pressure; instruct users to install the PWA (installed apps share the same quota and daily use defeats the 7-day eviction).
- Preprocess for OCR server-side: deskew + illumination flattening + adaptive (Sauvola-style) binarization for thermal receipts before Tesseract-class engines (>=300 DPI equivalent, ~10 px borders); if using Google Vision/Document AI, send the original grayscale JPEG (>=1024x768, <=20 MB) and let the service handle binarization — keep both original and normalized images.
- Test matrix to lock in CI/manual QA: iOS Safari browser-tab vs installed-PWA (permission re-prompts, stream death on backgrounding), an iOS<18 device for canvas caps, a 3-4 GB RAM Android (Redmi/Realme class) for WASM init time and OOM, Slack/Gmail in-app browsers on both platforms (file-input must carry these), and Samsung devices for EXIF/landscape-sensor orientation.

**UX patterns:**
- Native-camera file input as universal baseline (<input type=file accept='image/*' capture='environment'>): used by Expensify/Wave web flows; hands focus, HDR, flash, stabilization and orientation to the OS camera app, works in every in-app browser and on iOS without permission-re-prompt pain; the trade-off is no live edge overlay and no multi-shot batch ergonomics — works because it deletes the entire class of getUserMedia quirks for the 80% path.
- True-ratio viewfinder: render the stream with object-fit:cover full-bleed, overlay a receipt-shaped guide frame, and capture from a canvas sized to video.videoWidth x videoHeight (MDN pattern) — optionally crop with cover math so what-you-see-is-what-you-capture; fails (stretched receipts) whenever teams instead size the canvas from CSS pixels or force fill.
- Capability-gated controls: probe track.getCapabilities() and only render torch/zoom/focus buttons when present (Android Chrome); hiding torch on iOS avoids dead UI since WebKit ignored the constraint for years (bug 243075).
- Live quad highlight + auto-capture on steadiness: Scanbot 'Automatic capture when the device is optimally positioned' and Dynamsoft Smart Capture fire the shot when corners are stable, eliminating tap-induced blur; jscanify achieves the overlay at ~10 fps but leaves auto-fire logic to you — works because most blurry receipts come from the capture tap itself.
- Manual corner-adjust rescue editor after auto-detect (Dynamsoft DocumentCorrectionView pattern): when detection misses (white receipt on white desk, cluttered table), the user drags four handles; every commercial scanner ships this because pure auto-detection fails on exactly the receipts that matter (crumpled thermal paper).
- Pre-OCR quality gate: score sharpness/lighting before accepting the page (Scanbot DQA grades excellent->very poor); a DIY variance-of-Laplacian + brightness histogram threshold with a 'Retake' prompt catches unusable OCR input at capture time instead of after a failed claim — note even Scanbot's cannot detect bad cropping.
- One-stream multi-page loop: keep a single persistent getUserMedia stream across pages (iOS kills the first stream when a second is requested — bug 179363), append per-page thumbnails to a tray with add-page/retake, and store each page as a separate IndexedDB record tied to a draft submission id.
- Queue-state honesty on iOS: because Safari has no Background Sync, show an explicit 'N receipts waiting to upload' chip with per-item retry state, replay on app-open/online/visibilitychange (Workbox replays on SW startup) — and never promise 'uploads in background' copy on iOS; this dovetails with the soft-paywall pattern of accepting-and-queueing uploads when credits are exhausted.
- Permission pre-prompt education screen (explain why camera is needed before the first getUserMedia call): cuts denial rates and softens iOS standalone-PWA re-prompt fatigue documented in Apple Communities; pair with avoiding hash-route changes during the capture flow (bug 215884 trigger).
- EXIF-safe ingestion: route every file-input image through createImageBitmap/canvas re-encode so orientation is baked in and stripped, sidestepping the rotated-receipt bug class across Samsung/landscape-default sensors; getUserMedia captures never need it.

**Risks:**
- iOS Safari is the permanent ceiling: no ImageCapture (max still = stream resolution, device-dependent up to ~4K), no Background Sync, no focus control, unreliable torch, and an OPEN bug re-prompting camera permission in installed PWAs on hash navigation (WebKit 215884) — UX parity with native scanning apps is not achievable on iOS; design the flow so the file-input path feels first-class there.
- The 720p-iOS claim and several constraint behaviors in circulating blog posts are outdated (pre-iOS 13); conversely some 'Safari supports torch since iOS 15' claims in SEO content are wrong per WebKit's own tracker — every camera capability must be feature-detected at runtime via getCapabilities(), never UA-assumed.
- OpenCV.js-based free scanning (jscanify) carries hidden costs: ~8 MB download on metered Indian data plans, seconds of init on 4 GB devices, endemic Mat memory leaks, and documented detection failure without solid-background contrast — exactly the crumpled thermal receipt on a cafe table case. Budget for the manual-crop editor as the real workhorse.
- Quad-based perspective correction assumes a planar document: crumpled/curled thermal receipts violate this and dewarping is research-grade; long receipts exceed one frame and stitching is hard — multi-shot capture with overlap is the honest product answer.
- Vendor pricing risk: Scanbot does not publish prices (Capterra: 'Contact vendor'); the $1,249/yr Dynamsoft figure is a published starting point that scales with products/domains; treat commercial-SDK TCO as quote-dependent in the dossier.
- Blog-sourced OCR-preprocessing gains (15-30%) lack published methodology; only the Tesseract-official guidance (300 DPI, deskew, noise) is citable as fact — run your own A/B on Indian receipt corpora (thermal paper, bilingual English/Hindi) before claiming accuracy numbers.
- Safari's 7-day script-storage eviction can silently delete queued receipt blobs for infrequent users browsing in-tab; mitigation (prompt PWA install, upload eagerly, server-ack before deleting local copy) must be designed in, not bolted on.
- HEIC edge cases remain: Files-app picks and AirDropped images can arrive as real HEIC despite accept='image/*', and Safari 17 had conversion oddities — validate magic bytes server-side and keep a heic-decode fallback (or reject with a clear message).
- In-app browsers on iOS <14.5 (and some Android OEM webviews) lack getUserMedia entirely; B2B users opening approval links from Slack/Gmail will hit this — the capture entry point must detect and route to file-input silently.
- Workbox queues only on network exceptions, not 4xx/5xx: the soft-paywall 'accept and queue when credits exhausted' behavior must be implemented at the application layer (server accepts upload, marks unprocessed), not via the offline-retry queue, or paused-processing uploads will retry forever.


### [ocr-itemization-ux]
Best-in-class receipt capture splits into two latency regimes: real-time on-device capture coaching (edge/blur/glare detection, auto-torch, auto-capture — Veryfi Lens, Klippa SDK, Google Drive scanner) that must feel instant, and cloud extraction that every serious vendor treats as ASYNC with an explicit "Scanning" state, letting the employee background the app and surfacing results via notification or a human-assist chat (Expensify Concierge). Vendor-claimed extraction latencies cluster at 0.5-5 seconds (Klippa 0.5-4s, Fyle "within seconds", Veryfi sub-5s), but Expensify's own docs historically admitted "a few minutes to a few hours," and its Concierge human-fallback plus the always-available "Fill out details myself" escape hatch is the canonical HITL pattern: OCR is a draft, never a gate. Field-level review is converging on confidence-threshold routing (Azure Document Intelligence's documented >=0.80 straight-through convention) plus interactive bounding boxes that link each field to its image region, with Rossum's validation screen (blue box = machine, green only after human confirms, Tab jumps to low-confidence fields) as the reference implementation. On small screens, line items are progressive-disclosure: Zoho hides itemization behind an "Itemize" button next to Amount with per-line category and per-line tax auto-summing the total, while Splitwise/splitty show tap-an-item pickers for assignment. Failure UX is a recoverable queue, not a dead end: Zoho keeps a filterable "Scan Failed Receipts" bucket with bulk "Retry Autoscan" and per-receipt "add manually," and its iOS app even has a "plan limit exceeded" filter for re-uploading receipts once scan credits return — direct precedent for the proposed soft paywall. Duplicate handling has three tiers: capture-time frame dedupe (Google Drive scanner), submission-time dismissible warnings keyed on date+amount+currency with Keep-all/Keep-one resolution (Expensify), and auto-merge with tolerances (Fyle: amount epsilon 0.0002, date window 5 days). Email forwarding is table stakes (per-user unique address prefix@expense.zoho.com at Zoho; typo-tolerant receipts@brex.com; receipts@expensify.com; text-to-shortcode at Ramp/Expensify), and in India WhatsApp-in capture (Happay) replaces SMS. For Indian GST bills, Section 170 CGST Act mandates normal rounding to the nearest rupee per tax component per invoice, CGST/SGST must split equally intrastate, and Happay vendor-claims 92% OCR accuracy including GST extraction — while mainstream autoscan language lists (Zoho's 14-15) include zero Indic scripts even though Google Cloud Vision has 9 GA Indic languages, leaving a vernacular gap. Metering precedents validate the submissions-based pricing: Expensify free tier = 25 SmartScans/month with $0.20 overage; Zoho free = 20 autoscans, Standard = 20/user/month.

**Recommendations:**
- Architect the scan flow as two latency tiers per Nielsen limits: on-device capture coaching (edge/blur/glare/auto-crop) must respond <100ms-1s; cloud line-item extraction should be async with an explicit 'Scanning' chip on the draft expense, allow navigate-away/submit-later, and push-notify on completion — never block the form on OCR (the documented Expensify complaint).
- Target P50 extraction <5s (Klippa 0.5-4s and Fyle 'within seconds' prove feasible); if processing exceeds ~10s, switch from spinner to determinate progress + 'we'll notify you' per NN/g guidance.
- Adopt confidence-threshold review: auto-accept fields >=0.80 (Azure convention), visually flag only doubtful fields, and require explicit confirmation only of the three traceability fields (merchant, date, total — Expensify's mandatory trio) before submission.
- Implement Rossum-grade tap-to-fix: tapping any extracted field scrolls/zooms the receipt image to its bounding box; machine values render neutral, turn 'confirmed' (green) only on human touch; on mobile, edit line items in a focused bottom row-sheet (Zoho's Itemize pattern), not an inline grid.
- Build the failure path as a first-class queue clone of Zoho: 'Scan failed' bucket with per-receipt reasons (blurry, multi-receipt image, unsupported format, credits exhausted), bulk Retry, and one-tap 'enter manually' that pre-fills whatever was extracted (total/date/merchant) so manual line-item entry starts warm; remember merchant->category mappings per tenant for smart defaults.
- Mirror Zoho's 'plan limit exceeded' filter for the soft paywall: queued receipts remain visible with a 'processing paused — credits exhausted' state, an employee-side 'nudge admin' action, and automatic re-processing when credits are purchased (Zoho's retry-after-limit flow is the exact precedent).
- Duplicate strategy in three layers: capture-time perceptual-hash dedupe (Google Drive pattern), submission-time dismissible warning on date+amount+currency collision with 'Keep both'/'Keep one' verbs (Expensify), and silent auto-merge only with tight tolerances (Fyle's amount-epsilon + date-window) — and publish the rule like Brex publishes its 2-of-3 contract.
- Ship email-in receipts at launch (per-company or per-user unique address, Zoho-style editable prefix) and plan WhatsApp-in for India fast-follow (Happay precedent); SMS shortcodes are a US pattern, WhatsApp is the Indian analog.
- Model Indian GST natively in the line-item schema: per-line taxable value, rate, CGST/SGST (equal intrastate) or IGST, optional HSN/SAC and supplier GSTIN, an inclusive/exclusive toggle to reconcile sum-of-lines vs printed total, and Section 170 normal rounding (per component, per invoice) so recomputed totals match the bill to the rupee; validate CGST==SGST and flag mismatches instead of silently 'fixing'.
- Differentiate on vernacular: route OCR through an engine with GA Indic support (Cloud Vision's 9 GA languages) since incumbents' autoscan language lists omit Indic scripts entirely; regardless, keep the manual line-item fallback fully usable in low-literacy contexts (numeric keypad first, category icons, today-as-default date).
- Accessibility: pair auto-capture with audio/haptic edge feedback (Voice Dream Scanner pattern), label every capture control for screen readers, and ensure the entire review/fix flow is operable without touching the image (field list fallback when bounding-box interaction is unavailable).
- Pricing sanity: Expensify (25 free scans, $0.20 overage) and Zoho (20 free autoscans, 20/user/month on Standard) set the market's mental model for scan metering — a per-submission meter with unlimited seats is consistent with, and simpler than, both.

**UX patterns:**
- Async extraction with an explicit 'Scanning' expense state (Expensify): the expense object exists immediately with the receipt image and a SCANNING placeholder; user can close the app and is notified/Concierge-messaged later. Works because capture is committed instantly (employee trusts it's 'in') while slow OCR never blocks them. Anti-pattern observed: Capterra reviewers complaining OCR 'pauses your workflow' when it blocks.
- Vendor-side human fallback as chat (Expensify Concierge): when OCR can't find one of the 3 mandatory fields (merchant/date/amount), a named assistant messages the employee asking only for the missing field. Works because it converts silent failure into a scoped, conversational micro-task.
- Permanent 'Fill out details myself' escape hatch on every scanned receipt (Expensify): user can override any field at any time without waiting for or fighting the OCR. Works because OCR is framed as a draft, not a gate.
- Real-time capture coaching in the viewfinder (Klippa SDK: 'too far away', 'not enough lighting'; Veryfi Lens: blur/warp checks, <30ms ambient-light analysis with auto-torch): fixes image quality at the source, which is the single biggest OCR-accuracy lever; cheaper than any post-hoc correction UX.
- Auto-capture + Auto-Best Frame + capture-time duplicate skip (Google Drive scanner, on-device): hover-to-scan removes shutter-button judgement, blurry frames are silently swapped for the sharpest from the session, and already-captured pages are skipped before upload. Works because the user never sees (or pays for) a bad or duplicate frame.
- Confirm-the-crop-then-filter manual flow (CamScanner, Microsoft Lens legacy): auto edge detection draws an adjustable quadrilateral, then a default enhancement filter (Magic Color) with 4 alternatives. Works as the universally-learned fallback when auto-capture misfires; employees already know this gesture vocabulary.
- Long-receipt panoramic stitching and multi-doc capture (Veryfi Lens: stitch elongated thermal receipts, up to 4 documents per frame): handles the physically awkward receipts (grocery/pharmacy tapes) that single-frame capture mangles.
- Failure as a recoverable queue, not a dead end (Zoho Expense): failed scans land in a filterable 'Scan Failed Receipts' bucket with per-item 'add manually'/'delete' and bulk 'Retry Autoscan'; a parallel 'plan limit exceeded' filter lets receipts uploaded beyond scan credits be re-submitted for autoscan later — the exact soft-paywall queue the proposed product needs.
- Confidence-threshold review routing (Azure Document Intelligence guidance, Nanonets): auto-accept fields >=0.80 confidence, surface only doubtful fields for human confirmation. Works because it minimizes taps — users confirm 2-3 fields, not 12 — and concentrates attention where the model is weakest.
- Interactive bounding boxes linking field to image region (Rossum): clicking a field highlights its source box on the receipt; blue = machine-extracted, green ONLY after human validation; Tab jumps to next low-confidence field; ALT reveals all candidate boxes; dragging a box re-extracts from a new region. Works because spatial grounding lets users verify in one glance instead of re-reading the whole receipt.
- Progressive-disclosure itemization ('Itemize' button beside the Amount field, Zoho Expense): the simple one-amount expense stays one field; tapping Itemize opens line rows each with its own category and tax, and the header total becomes computed (sum of lines + taxes). Works on small screens because itemization is opt-in and each line edits in its own focused row rather than a dense grid.
- Tap-item-to-assign picker over extracted lines (Splitwise Pro, splitty): scan produces a tappable list of items; tapping toggles assignment to people; tax/tip distribute proportionally after assignment (splitty). Transferable as tap-to-set-category/tap-to-mark-personal per line in expense apps; works because it's direct manipulation on recognized objects, zero typing.
- Reply-with-photo capture in messaging (Brex SMS/WhatsApp, Ramp text HIRAMP 447-267, Happay WhatsApp in India): the system prompts at transaction/submission time and the employee replies with a photo, no app or login. Works because it meets employees in the channel they already have open; for India, WhatsApp is the analog of US SMS shortcodes.
- Transparent match contract (Brex: receipt auto-attaches when 2 of 3 of date/amount/merchant match; only receipts <2 months old auto-match): publishing the rule reduces 'where did my receipt go' support tickets and makes mismatches predictable and explainable.
- Per-user unique forwarding email with editable prefix (Zoho yourprefix@expense.zoho.com) vs single global typo-tolerant address (receipts@brex.com accepting misspellings, receipts@expensify.com): both eliminate sender-identification friction; the unique-address variant avoids 'forwarded from unrecognized email' failures, the global variant is easier to remember and print on policy docs.
- Duplicate warning as a dismissible violation with explicit resolution verbs (Expensify: red 'Potential duplicate' on BOTH expenses, hold applied, 'Keep all' vs 'Keep this one') layered under auto-merge with tolerance windows (Fyle: auto-merge when amount delta <=0.0002 and date delta <=5 days; card-linked/submitted expense wins): warn when uncertain, silently fix when provably the same transaction.
- Merchant-memory smart defaults (Zoho: first expense from a new merchant needs manual categorization, subsequent ones auto-categorize): per-tenant learned defaults make the manual-entry fallback faster every week without any model training.
- Skip capture entirely via supplier-side data (Brex auto-generated itemized receipts from merchant integrations 1-3 days post-transaction; Expensify eReceipts for card expenses under $75): the best scan UX is no scan; for a no-card India product the analog is email-in + future GSTN/e-invoice ingestion.
- Non-visual capture affordances (Voice Dream Scanner: tone steadies as all four edges enter frame, auto-capture, fixed swipe-count to Discard/Save; Supersense: voice guidance for aiming): pair auto-capture with audio/haptic edge feedback and fully labeled controls so blind and low-vision employees can self-serve receipts.
- Confidence-ordered field confirmation lists (industry baseline distilled from Expensify's 3 mandatory fields + Azure thresholding): always ask the user to confirm Amount, Date, Merchant first — they're mandatory for a traceable claim — and treat category, tax split, and line items as below-the-fold optional confirmations.

### [usage-pricing]
The proposed model (unlimited users/approvers free, meter = submissions, prepaid credit packs, queue-not-block soft paywall) is strongly supported by 2024-2026 market evidence. DocuSign envelopes are the closest structural analogue (per-unit allowance, pay-as-you-go overage, and a documented batching loophole the product must guard against); Intercom Fin ($0.99/outcome, 50 included, charge-once-per-conversation, hard limits) is the best template for outcome-fairness rules; OpenAI/Anthropic prepaid credits ($5 minimum, auto-recharge threshold, 12-month expiry) are the canonical prepaid-drawdown mechanics. Hybrid usage models dominate pure pay-as-you-go (OpenView: 45% of SaaS had UBP by 2022, ~61% some form by end-2023, with hybrids ~3x pure PAYG), and usage-pricing companies report roughly 10-point-higher NRR (~120% vs ~110%), which is the strategic payoff of metering submissions instead of seats. India-specific evidence is unusually favorable to prepaid metering: >90% of mobile users in most telecom circles are prepaid (recharge-pack mental model), WhatsApp Business API already bills Indian SMBs per message in INR (utility Rs 0.145, marketing Rs ~0.86), and Tally's perpetual-license + optional TSS renewal proves Indians accept "software keeps working, premium value lapses" — exactly the value-withheld soft paywall proposed. The end-user-nudges-admin loop is a documented, mainstream pattern: Slack hides (not deletes) history for every member until upgrade, Figma members request seats with 3-day temporary access, Notion members hit a "Request" button that notifies the workspace owner, and Zapier holds (not loses) over-limit task runs for replay — together these justify "uploads queued, processing paused, employee one-tap nudge to admin." The biggest risks are batching/gaming (multiple bills stuffed into one submission, mirroring DocuSign envelope-stuffing), monthly-expiry resentment (HubSpot credits expiring monthly is widely criticized; use 12-month validity instead), auto-top-up surprise charges (OpenAI community complaints), and the ethical/UX tension that a paused queue delays employees' own reimbursement money — mitigated by a permanent free monthly trickle plus burn-rate forecasts and 50/80/95% alerts (bill shock drives churn: ~73% of bill-shocked customers churn within 90 days per Recurly-cited data). Against verified COGS of roughly Rs 1-2.5 per submission (Google Document AI expense parser $0.01/page ≈ Rs 0.85, OCR read tier $1.50/1k pages, trivial storage), a credit ladder from Rs 10/submission (small packs) declining to Rs 5 (large packs, ~2x decline like Clay's $75→$16 per 1k credits curve) yields 67-85% gross margin and undercuts Zoho Expense's per-seat anchor (Rs 99/user/month, min 5 users, only 20 autoscans/user) for any company averaging under ~14 submissions per user per month — i.e., virtually all Indian SMBs.

**Recommendations:**
- Meter definition: 1 credit = 1 accepted employee expense submission (one report), inclusive of OCR on up to 5 receipt images, all approval-flow actions, resubmissions after rejection, and manual-entry fallback. Images beyond 5 in a single submission consume 1 additional credit per 5 — a fair-use guard against DocuSign-style batching, stated plainly in pricing FAQ.
- Free forever tier: unlimited users/approvers + 20 free submissions per company per month (resets monthly, no rollover). Rationale: mirrors Zapier 100 tasks / Clay 100 credits / Zoho 20 autoscans; keeps a 5-person pilot free indefinitely while a 25-person company exhausts it in week one.
- Prepaid credit ladder (1 credit = 1 submission; ex-GST; UPI/netbanking/card; 12-month validity from purchase; non-refundable; FIFO drawdown; oldest-first): Top-up Rs 499 = 50 credits (Rs 9.98/credit); Starter Rs 1,999 = 250 (Rs 8.00); Growth Rs 6,999 = 1,000 (Rs 7.00); Scale Rs 24,999 = 4,500 (Rs 5.56); Enterprise Rs 74,999 = 15,000 (Rs 5.00, adds invoice billing + priority support). ~2x unit-price decline top-to-bottom, matching Clay/Postmark curves while staying simple.
- Margin arithmetic: COGS ≈ Rs 1.0-2.5/submission (Document AI Expense Parser Rs 0.85/page or OCR-read+LLM Rs 0.2-0.8; storage <Rs 0.05/month/image; compute/bandwidth Rs 0.2-0.5) → gross margin 67-75% at Rs 5/credit, 80-90% at Rs 8-10/credit. Never price below Rs 4/credit even in enterprise deals to preserve 50%+ margin headroom for payout-API costs in Phase 2.
- Competitive arithmetic to use in sales material: 25-employee company at 4 submissions/employee/month = 100 credits/month = Rs 700/month on Growth pack vs Zoho Expense Standard 25 × Rs 99 = Rs 2,475/month (and Zoho still caps autoscans at 20/user). Break-even density vs Zoho is ~14 submissions/user/month — far above realistic usage, so the submissions meter wins for almost every Indian SMB while 'unlimited users free' removes the seat-counting objection entirely.
- Soft paywall spec: at 0 credits, uploads and submissions continue to be accepted and stored (never lost — Zapier/Slack precedent), OCR + itemisation + approval routing pause into an 'Awaiting credits' queue, and processing auto-resumes FIFO on recharge. Employee sees a calm banner ('Your bills are safe; processing resumes when credits are added') with a one-tap 'Notify admin' (Notion/Figma request pattern). Admin sees aggregated nudge count + queue depth + one-click buy. Do NOT block viewing of past data or already-approved reports.
- Bill-shock/lockout prevention: admin alerts (email + WhatsApp utility message at Rs 0.145 + in-app) at 50/80/95/100% of balance; home-screen burn-rate forecast ('At ~120 submissions/week, credits last until ~July 3'); optional auto-top-up, default OFF, explicit opt-in with chosen pack + threshold (e.g., below 50 credits buy Growth), email receipt on every top-up and one-click disable — directly addressing OpenAI auto-recharge complaints.
- Expiry/rollover policy: 12-month validity (OpenAI/Anthropic norm) — avoid HubSpot-style monthly expiry which is the single most resented credit mechanic; within validity, credits are a drawdown balance so 'rollover' is moot. On bulk/enterprise annual deals, allow 15-20% carryover on renewal (m3ter/Clay precedent) as a negotiation lever.
- India packaging psychology: market packs as 'recharges' (telecom mental model, >90% prepaid familiarity); show per-submission effective price on each pack card; +18% GST shown the Tally/WhatsApp way; offer a 10% bonus-credit incentive on annual-sized prepay via UPI instead of a discount (bonus credits protect rate integrity); consider a Tally-style 'AMC' framing for an optional support+SLA annual add-on rather than discounting credits.
- Anti-pitfall rules to publish in the pricing FAQ: approvals/rejections/resubmissions never consume credits (Fin charge-once precedent); failed OCR never consumes an extra credit; queued submissions never expire or get deleted; credits are company-pooled (no per-user allocation politics); no negative balance, no surprise invoice — prepaid is the spend cap.
- Launch sequencing: start with the 5-pack ladder only (no subscription) to validate the meter; add an optional monthly auto-pack ('subscribe to Growth, get 1,100 credits/month' = 10% bonus) once cohorts show stable burn — this recreates the hybrid subscription+usage model that OpenView data says dominates, without launching two billing systems on day one.

**UX patterns:**
- Queue-not-block (Zapier 'held tasks'): over-limit work is held, never lost, and replayable after credits/upgrade — framed as a 'safety net for your data'; failure mode to avoid is silent holding, so pair with proactive alerts before and at exhaustion.
- Value-withheld, access-retained (Slack 90-day history): the product keeps accepting and storing everything; older value is hidden behind upgrade and 'revealed' on payment — every end user sees the loss, only the owner can fix it, creating distributed bottom-up pressure.
- Graceful degradation to read-only (Airtable over-limits badge, Trello view-only at >10 collaborators, Tally without TSS): core data always viewable, premium motion paused — Tally proves Indian SMBs culturally accept exactly this contract.
- Member-requests-upgrade with admin approval (Figma seats: manual/auto-approve settings + 3-day provisional access; Notion: 'Request' button → owner's inbox + 'Allow members to request upgrades' toggle; Miro: license requests routed by admin request-management settings) — template for the employee 'Notify admin to buy credits' one-tap nudge with admin-side aggregated request counts.
- Charge-once fairness rules (Intercom Fin): one conversation = max one charge; no charge on failure or human handoff — for expenses: one submission = one credit regardless of OCR retries, rejections, resubmissions, or approval steps.
- Prepaid drawdown with auto-top-up (OpenAI/Anthropic): buy credit balance upfront (min $5), optional auto-recharge at user-set threshold and amount, 12-month expiry, non-refundable; known trap: auto-recharge firing unexpectedly — make it opt-in with email receipt per top-up and a one-click off switch.
- Declining marginal price ladder (Clay $75→$16 per 1k credits; Postmark $1.80→$1.20 per 1k emails; Zapier slider; Twilio volume discounts): bigger packs cost less per unit, rewarding prepayment without negotiation.
- Included-allowance + overage hybrid (DocuSign 100 envelopes/user/yr then pay-as-you-go; Fin 50 included then $0.99; Zapier 1.25x pay-per-task): a base allowance absorbs normal usage, overage is dearer per unit than pack units — inverts to 'soft paywall + cheaper big packs' in a prepaid model.
- Burn-rate visibility and threshold alerts (industry standard per Alguna/Monetizely/Stigg: alerts at 60/80/90%, real-time dashboards, forecast 'you will run out ~the 18th at current burn'; Fin ships usage reminders + hard limits) — prepaid balance widget on admin home with projected exhaustion date.
- Rollover as trust signal, bounded (m3ter: full rollover rare; 10-20% carryover on renewal common; Clay banks up to 2x monthly; HubSpot's monthly expiry is the resented anti-pattern) — for prepaid packs, 12-month validity sidesteps monthly-expiry resentment entirely.
- Recharge-pack mental model (India telecom: >90% prepaid; WhatsApp BSPs sell INR message credits; Tally TSS annual renewal): position credit packs as 'recharges' with long validity, UPI payment, GST invoice — familiar to every Indian SMB owner.
- Unit-definition gaming (DocuSign envelope stuffing — many documents per envelope counts once): expect multi-bill batching into one submission; bound it with a fair-use cap (e.g., N receipt images per submission) rather than per-image pricing that reintroduces unit anxiety.

### [onboarding-config]
Across Zoho Expense, Concur, Fyle, Keka, Ramp and ApprovalMax, three approval-configuration paradigms dominate: per-user org-graph pointers (Zoho's "Submits To" / "Approves and Forwards To"), ordered rule stacks with an immovable fallback rule (Fyle), and amount/limit-based Delegation-of-Authority routing (Concur's level-based vs limit-based authorized approvers). The most instructive failure modes are documented in vendor KBs: Zoho hard-blocks submission when a claim matches no approval flow ("does not match your organization's approval criteria"), Concur reports get stuck with "Missing the required approver for next workflow step," and SAP KB 1944022 shows a self-approval deadlock (manager routed to themselves) that requires a support ticket to fix — so a v2 product should ship an always-matching fallback rule, cycle detection on save, prevent-self-approval-with-skip-up default, and a "simulate this claim" tester (precedent: Oracle Fusion's Test Rules > Simulate against real transactions). For mid-flight policy changes, the industry pattern is snapshot-at-submission (Ramp: "Changes to approval workflows only apply to new requests, not those already in progress") with explicit admin-triggered restart (ApprovalMax) and workflow version history. Onboarding benchmarks argue for radical defaulting: median SaaS time-to-value is ~1.5 days, median activation 37%, and onboarding checklists average only 19.2% completion, so the wizard must get a first claim submitted in one session with preloaded categories, a size-based approval template, and everything else (GST, branches, bank file format, employee import) deferred to a checklist. Zoho's own onboarding (quick org setup, default categories, ZIA nudges, free 2-hour Jumpstart) and Fyle's invite flow (bulk Excel template, auto-invite emails, 24-hour links, Pending state, resend) are the closest concrete templates. India-specific config that must exist but can default: GSTIN optional at signup, INR + Apr–Mar fiscal year, branch entities each carrying their own GSTIN (Zoho supports up to 200 branches). Concur's configuration reputation supplies the differentiation story: G2/Capterra reviewers describe back-end configuration as consultant-dependent and "clunky... completed in an odd way that takes longer than you think it should," validating "first claim in 15 minutes, no consultant" as the wedge.

**Recommendations:**
- Recommended onboarding wizard (5 steps, <15 min, everything else deferred): (1) Create account with work email → org name, country=India default, currency INR, fiscal year Apr–Mar prefilled; GSTIN field present but skippable. (2) Pick a company-size template — '<50: single level, claims go to reporting manager'; '50–250: manager → finance, plus amount threshold (e.g., >Rs 25,000 adds finance head)'; '250+: manager → department head over threshold → finance', all editable later. (3) Categories preloaded (India set: Local Conveyance, Travel–Air/Rail, Lodging, Meals, Fuel/Mileage, Telecom, Office Supplies, Software/Subscriptions, Medical, Miscellaneous) with on/off checkboxes only. (4) Invite up to 3 people inline (name, email, manager) or 'skip — import later'. (5) A pre-seeded demo claim appears; admin approves it in-wizard (first value = watching a claim flow). Bank account for payout-file export, GST details, branches and bulk import live on a post-wizard checklist — none block the first claim.
- Hard-require before first REAL claim only three things: at least one admin, a guaranteed-match fallback approval rule (ship it built-in and undeletable, Fyle-style), and at least one active category. Bank details are required before the first PAYOUT RUN, not the first claim — sequence value before compliance chores.
- Build the approval matrix as a rule-builder with role abstractions (Manager, Manager's manager, Department head, Finance, named group) plus an optional one-screen limit grid (approver × max amount, Concur-style) for DoA-minded finance teams; avoid pure org-chart-only chains, but import 'manager email' from CSV to power the Manager role.
- Ship four safety invariants from day one: (a) graph validation (cycles, orphan approvers, deactivated-user references) runs on every user/rule save with inline errors; (b) prevent-self-approval ON by default with automatic skip-to-next-level (cite SAP KB 1944022 deadlock as the why); (c) vacation/out-of-office approver (approval-only, no account access) with optional auto-escalation after N days idle; (d) claims never blocked at submission for routing reasons — worst case routes to admin queue with a 'rule gap' flag.
- Implement 'Simulate a claim' in the matrix editor (pick employee + amount + category + entity → render the exact approver chain and which rule fired), Oracle-style but one click; also show the resolved chain to employees on the submission screen (Fyle pattern).
- Adopt snapshot-at-submission semantics and say so in the UI (copy Ramp's sentence); give admins an explicit 'Re-route in-flight claims to new policy' action that resets approvals (ApprovalMax restart pattern) and keep a who-changed-what version history of policies and matrices.
- Employee import: ship a downloadable CSV template (name, work email, employee ID, department, manager email, branch, role), an in-browser map→validate→fix grid with one-click fixes, dedupe on email + employee ID, and a dry-run preview ('212 will be invited, 3 duplicates skipped, 5 errors'); send invites with 7-day magic links (Fyle's 24h is too aggressive), Pending state, bulk resend. Add domain capture (DNS TXT verification → auto-join policy) so late employees self-onboard without admin work.
- Role set for Phase 1: Admin, Finance (approve + payout-file export + credits purchase), Approver (derived from rules, not assigned), Employee, and a read-only Auditor (view/comment/export-CSV only, Expensify-style) — keep all free per the unlimited-users pricing to make auditor/CA access a differentiator; defer custom roles to paid tiers like Zoho only if needed.
- Per-entity design: model Branch/Entity with its own GSTIN, address, bank account (for separate payout files) and optional approval-matrix override; provide 'copy configuration from existing branch' (ApprovalMax copy pattern) and cap complexity (Zoho's 200-branch ceiling shows even leaders bound this).
- Instrument onboarding against benchmarks: target first demo-claim approved in-session (minutes), first real claim < 24h (beats the 1.5-day median TTV), define activation = 3 events (first real claim submitted, first approval completed, first payout file exported) and expect ~19% checklist completion — so re-nudge via the employee-side soft-paywall nudges and ZIA-style assistant rather than relying on the checklist.
- Weaponize Concur's configuration reputation in positioning and design reviews: every config screen should pass the test 'could an Indian SMB office manager do this without a consultant or support ticket?' — the documented Concur failure modes (stuck 'missing required approver' reports, backend-change-only routing fixes) are the anti-checklist.

**UX patterns:**
- Ordered rule-stack with an immovable fallback rule (Fyle): every org has a default 'route to primary approver' rule that cannot be deleted, only re-ordered — structurally prevents orphan claims; contrast Zoho, which blocks submission outright when no custom flow matches. Adopt the Fyle invariant.

### [approval-ux]
Approver-side evidence confirms that manager review of expense reports is largely ceremonial today: GBTA pegs processing at $58/20 minutes per report with 19% containing errors, PayStream (via AppZen) found 60% of companies review only a sample, and AppZen documents managers rubber-stamping reports in ~15 seconds. Market leaders have responded not with faster rubber-stamping but with risk-tiered review: Expensify auto-approves compliant reports with a default 5% random audit and a Guided Review that surfaces only violations; Ramp auto-approves under thresholds and ships an AI Policy Agent that starts in review-only mode; Expensify's May-2025 bulk approvals are deliberately restricted to low-risk compliant reports, while Concur's lack of manager-level batch approval (Processor role only) is a long-standing complaint. For one-tap approvals, the pragmatic ladder is: deep-link magic buttons in plain HTML email (universal), then Outlook Actionable Messages for M365 shops (registration via Microsoft, single-user mailboxes only, legacy-token EOL June 8 2026), then Gmail AMP/one-click actions last (per-sender whitelisting, 100+ emails/day quality bar). Slack/Teams interactive cards (Fyle, Power Automate Adaptive Cards) demonstrate the decision-card pattern: amount, merchant/date, purpose, receipt flag, policy flags, plus Approve/Send-back, with in-place card refresh to prevent double action. WhatsApp is viable and cheap for India: approval nudges fit Meta's utility category (~Rs 0.115-0.145/message + 18% GST, free inside the 24h service window since July 1 2025), with up to 10 quick-reply buttons, but copy must stay strictly transactional or Meta recategorizes to marketing (~7x cost) without notice since April 16 2025. Delegation needs two distinct primitives (time-bound out-of-office auto-forward vs full delegate, per Zoho) plus admin-assignable delegates for extended leave/vacant managers — a documented Concur Standard gap. Escalation best practice converges on ~2 reminders then escalate (48h standard SLA, 24h high-value), inside working hours only. Maker-checker integrity demands send-back-with-required-comment, line-level partial rejection, and an immutable SOX-grade audit log (who/when/what/channel, submitter≠approver, ~7-year retention) — especially important once approvals move to one-tap channels.

**Recommendations:**
- Default approval policy template: auto-approve in-policy submissions under a per-company amount threshold, force manual review above it, and randomly route a configurable % (default 5%, Expensify precedent) of auto-approvals to human review — this is the only honest answer to the 60%-sample/15-second rubber-stamp reality while keeping maker-checker integrity.
- Build the approver queue as exceptions-first: pre-screen every submission (OCR mismatch, duplicate, missing receipt, policy flags) before it reaches the approver, badge each item approve-recommended / needs-review / reject-recommended, and offer guided review that steps only through flags. Never present a flat chronological list.
- Offer bulk approve only for zero-flag items (multi-select with flagged rows visibly excluded), available to the line-approver role — directly answering Concur's most-complained gap where bulk approval needs a back-office Processor role.
- One-tap channel strategy for India Phase 1: (1) signed single-use magic-link Approve / Send-back buttons in plain HTML email (universal, zero registration); (2) WhatsApp utility template nudges with a deep-link button to the specific report (Rs 0.115-0.145 + GST, free inside 24h CSW); (3) Outlook Actionable Messages only when an M365-heavy customer asks (org-scope registration in their tenant); (4) skip Gmail AMP at launch — the 100-emails/day-per-sender whitelisting bar and Gmail-only rendering don't fit an early-stage B2B sender.
- Keep WhatsApp approval templates strictly transactional ('Report #N for Rs X from <employee> awaits your approval') with buttons linking only to that report — any promotional copy or generic-landing-page button risks Meta's no-notice utility-to-marketing recategorization (~7x cost) in force since April 16, 2025.
- Make every one-tap action a single-use, expiring, amount-bounded token: card/email mutates to a decided state after use (Teams pattern), replay is rejected, and approvals above a configurable amount require step-up (open the web app) rather than one-tap — this is the control that lets one-tap coexist with maker-checker.
- Decision card spec (all channels, mobile-first): amount + currency, merchant + date, business purpose, receipt thumbnail (tap to zoom), policy flags with plain-language reasons, requester context (recent submission history, duplicate warnings, remaining budget if configured), and exactly two actions — Approve and Send back with required comment. Reject-with-reason and line-level reject live one tap deeper.
- Implement send-back-with-required-comment as the primary negative action (Concur/Zoho precedent) and line-level partial rejection (Zoho precedent) so one bad line never blocks a whole report; allow approver inline edits of trivial fields only with mandatory rationale, all logged.
- Ship two delegation primitives at launch: time-bound out-of-office approver (auto-forwards pending + future items, auto-expires, no account access) and admin-assigned delegate for extended leave or vacant-manager cases; add a company-level fallback approver so submissions never route to nobody, and surface unroutable items on the admin dashboard.
- Default reminder/escalation policy (admin-tunable): 48h approval SLA (24h for amounts above the high-value threshold), mid-SLA nudge, pre-escalation warning, then auto-escalate to backup/manager; cap at 2 reminders per item; deliver only within configured working hours (IST-aware quiet hours); batch multiple pending items into one digest per approver per day.
- Build the period-close sweep into the admin console: pending-approvals-by-approver dashboard with one-click remind-all and an approve-by date tied to the company's close calendar — finance chasing approvers is the #1 documented month-end pain.
- Write the audit log to SOX-grade spec from day one even for unlisted Indian SMBs: immutable append-only events capturing actor, role-at-decision-time, action, before/after values, timestamp, IP/device, and channel (web/email-link/WhatsApp/Slack); enforce submitter != approver at the engine level; retain 7-8 years. Channel provenance matters because one-tap approvals move decisions off-web.

**UX patterns:**
- Risk-tiered queue (Expensify Concierge, Ramp): auto-approve in-policy expenses under a configurable amount, randomly route a default ~5% of auto-approvals to manual review, and force manual review above a threshold — preserves maker-checker integrity via sampling instead of pretending every report gets read. Works because it matches reviewer attention to actual risk; the 60%-sample-review stat shows companies already do this informally.
- Guided review / exceptions-only review (Expensify Guided Review): the approver is stepped through ONLY flagged violations and warnings with per-item Reject/Edit/Skip; clean reports are approvable in one action. Fails when every field is shown with equal weight — that recreates the 15-second rubber stamp.
- Compliant-only bulk approve (Expensify May 2025, Zoho): multi-select approve is offered, but only for reports with zero unacknowledged violations; flagged reports are excluded from the bulk path. Concur's counter-example (bulk approval locked to back-office Processor role) is a top manager complaint.
- Decision card with the six decision fields (Fyle Slack, Teams Adaptive Cards): amount, merchant + date, business purpose, receipt thumbnail/attached flag, policy-violation flags, and requester context (history/budget) — plus exactly two primary actions: Approve and Send back. Anything requiring deeper digging links into the full web view.
- In-place card mutation after action (Teams Adaptive Cards, Gmail ConfirmAction): once acted on, the card re-renders as an 'Approved/Sent back' banner and buttons disappear; one-tap actions are single-use tokens so a decision cannot be replayed or double-fired from a forwarded message.
- Comment-on-decision affordance (Teams, Concur): tapping Reject/Send-back reveals a required comment field (Concur mandates the send-back comment; Zoho requires a rejection reason) — rationale is captured at the moment of decision and lands in the audit trail and the employee's notification.
- Line-level partial decision (Zoho selective expense rejection): approve the report while rejecting individual expense lines with reasons, so one bad line item does not block reimbursement of nine good ones; rejected lines return to the employee for fix-and-resubmit.
- Approver-edit-with-rationale (Zoho): let the approver fix trivial errors (category, project tag) inline with a mandatory 'reason for edit' rather than bouncing the report — cuts the 19%-error correction loop ($52/18min per GBTA) but keeps an attributable change log.
- Email approval ladder: ship deep-link 'magic button' approve/send-back links in plain HTML email first (works in every client, signed single-use URLs); add Outlook Actionable Messages for Microsoft-heavy customers (org-scope registration, single-user mailboxes only, Action.Execute on AC 1.4+); defer Gmail AMP/one-click actions (per-sender whitelisting, 100+ emails/day for weeks, no promo content) until volume justifies it.
- WhatsApp utility nudge with quick-reply deep link (India-specific): 'Report #142 (Rs 4,300, 3 items) is pending your approval' as a utility template with a URL button deep-linking to that specific report; strictly transactional copy keeps it at ~Rs 0.115-0.145 vs Rs 0.86 marketing rate and avoids Meta's no-notice recategorization (active since April 16, 2025). Replies open a free 24h service window for follow-up messages.
- Two-nudges-then-escalate cadence: SLA per approval class (48h default, 24h high-value), one mid-SLA reminder, one pre-escalation warning (~24h before), then automatic reroute up the ladder primary approver -> backup/delegate -> approver's manager or admin queue; all reminders sent inside working hours with quiet-hours suppression and multiple pendings batched into a single digest.
- Dual delegation primitives (Zoho model): time-bound Out-of-office Approver (auto-forwards pending items, no account access, auto-expires) for vacations, and a standing Delegate (acts on behalf, fully audit-attributed as 'X approved as delegate of Y') for EAs/finance; plus admin-assignable delegation for extended leave — the documented Concur Standard gap where admins cannot reassign an absent approver's queue.
- Vacant-manager fallback routing: when 'submits-to' resolves to nobody (manager left, new hire unassigned), route to a configurable fallback (department head or finance admin queue) instead of silently queuing; surface unroutable submissions on the admin dashboard. (Recommendation pattern — vendors document hierarchy-based routing but handle vacancy poorly.)
- Period-close sweep (month-end): a pre-close dashboard of pending approvals grouped by approver with one-click 'remind all' and an approve-by date, so finance sees exactly who is blocking close; compressed close windows demonstrably raise error rates.
- Pre-screen before human review (Expensify AI receipt detection, Ramp Policy Agent): OCR/policy engine flags duplicates, AI-generated receipts, missing fields, and out-of-policy lines BEFORE the approver opens the item, and an AI recommender labels each item approve/review/reject in review-only mode first — the approver starts from a triaged queue, not a raw one.
- Unified approvals inbox (Zoho 'Pending Approval' tab): one queue across reports/advances/requests sorted by risk and age (not just recency), with face-up policy flags — prevents email-buried approvals, the most-cited cause of stuck reimbursements.
- Forward/reassign single approval (Zoho Forward button): an approver who lacks context can hand one specific report to a better-placed approver with a note, without reconfiguring the whole workflow — keeps maker-checker intact because the forward is logged as part of the chain.

### [pwa-india]
For an India-first employee bill-capture product, the PWA path is viable and strategically correct, because the install base is overwhelmingly Android (93.2% vs 6.7% iOS in India, May 2026, StatCounter) and Android PWAs are near-native: Chrome mints a real WebAPK (app drawer, settings, URL intent capture), the service-worker requirement for install was dropped in Chrome 108, web push works, and an installed PWA can register as a Web Share Target to receive bill photos shared from WhatsApp or the gallery (Chrome 76+, Android-only). The genuinely load-bearing engineering decision is the camera: getUserMedia delivers only video-stream resolution frames, ImageCapture.takePhoto() (full still resolution) is Chromium 59+ and only reached Safari in 18.4 (March 2025), and a long-lived WebKit bug (252465) still intermittently blacks out getUserMedia streams in iOS standalone PWAs through iOS 18.4.1 — so the primary capture path must be <input type=file capture="environment"> invoking the native camera app at full sensor resolution, with live-preview edge detection as progressive enhancement only. iOS is the risk concentration but a small one for this ICP: no install prompt (share-sheet A2HS only), push only for installed Home Screen apps since iOS 16.4, no Background Sync, no share target, storage isolated between Safari and the installed app (forcing re-login), and Apple demonstrated platform risk by briefly killing EU Home Screen web apps in iOS 17.4 beta before reversing on March 1, 2024 (rest-of-world, including India, was never affected). Offline-first is mandatory and well-patterned: an IndexedDB outbox with Workbox background-sync replay (with SW-startup fallback for Safari), navigator.storage.persist(), and generous quotas (Chrome 60% of disk per origin; Safari 17 gives Home Screen web apps the same quota as the browser, and installed apps are exempt from the 7-day script-writable-storage purge). Data cost is no longer the binding constraint in India (~$0.09/GB, 27.5→31GB/month average usage) — network consistency is (even Jio's best-in-class Consistent Quality score is only 66.5%, i.e. roughly a third of demanding tests like image upload fail thresholds), which argues for client-side compression and queued upload UX, not data austerity theatrics. Low-end Android (4-8GB RAM is the largest segment at ~42%) imposes real budgets: ~150KiB HTML/CSS + 300-350KiB JS gzipped for a 5-second P75 first load, iOS canvas caps (16.78M pixels per canvas, ~384MB total canvas memory) and ~300MB practical WASM allocation ceilings mean heavy document-scanner processing should default to the server with on-device WASM as an opt-in for capable devices. Vernacular UI is non-negotiable for field staff (98% of India's 886M internet users consume Indic-language content; 55% of users are rural), and the web stack ships language updates instantly without store review. The staged recommendation: (1) responsive web with file-input capture works for 100% of devices including iOS Safari day one; (2) installable PWA adds the Android-native experience (WebAPK, push, share target, offline outbox) for ~93% of Indian users; (3) wrap the same PWA as a TWA via Bubblewrap when customers demand Play Store presence (mind the July 2024 spam/minimum-functionality policy — TWAs of your own quality PWA are officially supported, thin webviews are removed); (4) only reach for Capacitor if iOS push reliability or native camera UX becomes a paid-deal blocker, since it buys APNs push without the Home-Screen-install precondition and a native camera plugin at the cost of app-store review overhead. Indian precedent (Flipkart Lite's 70% conversion lift from home-screen users; Ola's 200KB PWA growing tier-2/3 traffic 68%) proves the model for exactly this user class, even though those canonical numbers are 2016-17 vendor-marketing era; notably, no leading Indian field-force or expense vendor leads with a PWA today, which is both a gap and a caution.

### [v-camera] Mobile getUserMedia honors resolution/aspect constraints only loosely; stretched-bill distortion is a CSS/canvas scaling mismatch vs true videoWidth/videoHeight
- Verdict: **partially-confirmed** (high)
- Corrected: As of June 2026: (1) CONFIRMED — getUserMedia constraints are requests, not guarantees: bare/ideal values are explicitly non-required and the browser returns the closest supported mode (only exact/min/max are mandatory and can throw OverconstrainedError), so mobile streams routinely differ from the requested resolution/aspect (MDN). (2) CONFIRMED — the delivered frames themselves are undistorted at the stream's intrinsic videoWidth×videoHeight; stretching arises in presentation/capture: object-fit:fill "stretched to fit" when box aspect mismatches (MDN), or a canvas bitmap sized from the layout box instead of videoWidth/videoHeight. Fully fixable in web code via the canonical pattern: request ideal constraints -> wait for metadata and read video.videoWidth/videoHeight -> preview with explicit CSS box + object-fit:cover (crops, never stretches) -> capture with canvas.width=video.videoWidth, canvas.height=video.videoHeight, ctx.drawImage(video,0,0,w,h); for WYSIWYG against a cover-cropped preview use 9-arg drawImage with a source-rect crop matching the box aspect (MDN "Taking still photos" + drawImage). (3) OUTDATED on the Safari half — takePhoto is a true photographic exposure ("highest available photographic camera resolution", Chrome; W3C spec distinguishes it from grabFrame's video-resolution snapshot) and has shipped in Chrome 59+ (Android+desktop, 2017); but WebKit shipped the Image Capture API in Safari 18.4 (iOS 18.4, Mar 31 2025: takePhoto, getPhotoCapabilities, getPhotoSettings) and grabFrame in Safari 26.0 (Sep 2025) — so "iOS Safari no" was true only through iOS 18.3; note caniuse's curated page still lags WebKit's own release notes here, and Firefox remains flag-gated/unsupported. (4) CONFIRMED — getUserMedia works in installed (standalone/home-screen) web apps since iOS 13.4 (Mar 2020), per WebKit bug 185448 (broken from iOS 11.3-13.3). (5) HALF-OUTDATED — Android Chrome supports torch/zoom (since Chrome 59 via applyConstraints) and focusMode/focusDistance; on iOS, zoom shipped in Safari 17.0 (Sep 18 2023, exposed in MediaTrackCapabilities and constrainable), whiteBalanceMode in Safari 17.4 (Mar 5 2024), and torch was implemented Oct 2023 and confirmed working by iOS 17.5.1 (getSettings staleness fixed in Safari 18.4); only manual focus (focusMode/focusDistance) remains unsupported on iOS Safari as of Safari 26.x.
