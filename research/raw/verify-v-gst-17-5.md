# VERIFY: Under GST Section 17(5), input tax credit is BLOCKED on certain employee/T&E expenses (food & beverages, club membership, travel benefits to employees, rent-a-cab, motor vehicles), with key exceptions: obligatory under law; same-category onward supply; for making outward taxable supply.

- **Verdict:** confirmed  (confidence: high)
- **Corrected / best-supported:** CONFIRMED with precision notes. Section 17(5) of the CGST Act, 2017 does block ITC on these items, but they sit in DIFFERENT clauses and the claim slightly conflates them: (1) Motor vehicles for transport of persons with approved seating capacity of NOT MORE THAN THIRTEEN persons (including driver) are blocked under clause (a) — NOT under the employee-expense clause (b). Exceptions: (A) further supply of such vehicles, (B) transportation of passengers, (C) imparting driving training. Insurance/servicing/repair/maintenance of such vehicles is blocked under clause (ab). (2) Food & beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic/plastic surgery, LEASING/RENTING/HIRING of motor vehicles (i.e. 'rent-a-cab'), and life & health insurance are blocked under clause (b)(i). (3) Membership of a club, health and fitness centre — clause (b)(ii). (4) Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation such as leave or home travel concession (LTC/HTC) — clause (b)(iii). Note 'travel benefits' is NARROW: it means vacation/leave/home-travel concessions, NOT ordinary business travel — ITC on business travel (e.g. air/rail for work) is generally available and not blocked by 17(5). EXCEPTIONS (correct, but the scoping differs): (i) the proviso under (b)(i) [the food/insurance/rent-a-cab group] allows ITC where the inward supply is used for making an outward TAXABLE supply of the SAME CATEGORY, or as an element of a taxable composite/mixed supply (e.g. a restaurant buying food, a tour operator hiring cabs onward); (ii) the proviso after the whole of clause (b) allows ITC on ALL clause (b) items where it is OBLIGATORY for an employer to provide them to employees under any law for the time being in force (this broad obligatory-under-law exception was extended to the entire clause (b) by the CGST Amendment Act 2018, effective 1 Feb 2019; previously it was narrower). The 'thirteen persons' threshold itself also dates from the 1 Feb 2019 amendment (earlier wording was about specified uses without the seat count).

## Reasoning

I verified the exact statutory text against the official CBIC tax repository (taxinformation.cbic.gov.in), whose verbatim snippet for clause (b) reads: '(i) food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, cosmetic and plastic surgery, leasing, renting or hiring of motor vehicles, vessels or aircraft referred to in clause (a) or clause (aa) except when used for the purposes specified therein, life insurance and health insurance: Provided that the input tax credit ... shall be available where an inward supply ... is used by a registered person for making an outward taxable supply of the same category of goods or services or both or as an element of a taxable composite or mixed supply; (ii) membership of a club, health and fitness centre; and (iii) travel benefits extended to employees on vacation such as leave or home travel concession: Provided that the input tax credit ... shall be available, where it is obligatory for an employer to provide the same to its employees under any law for the time being [in force].' Clause (a) blocking motor vehicles for transport of persons with approved seating capacity of not more than thirteen persons (including driver), with exceptions for further supply / passenger transport / driving training, is confirmed by IndianKanoon's reproduction and corroborated by ClearTax. The claim is directionally correct and lists the right items and the three exception themes, but as an adversarial check I flag two real imprecisions a feasibility study should not gloss over: (a) it conflates clause (a) motor vehicles with the clause (b) employee/T&E items — relevant because the seat-count test and its vehicle-specific exceptions differ from the food/insurance/club/travel-benefit rules; and (b) 'travel benefits to employees' in the statute is specifically vacation/leave/home-travel concession, not general business T&E — ordinary business-travel ITC is not blocked by 17(5), an important nuance for an expense-reimbursement product. The two provisos also have different scope (same-category-outward-supply applies to the (b)(i) group; obligatory-under-law applies to all of clause (b) post 1 Feb 2019), which the claim's flat listing obscures.
