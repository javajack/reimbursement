# VERIFY: A SaaS that generates bank payout files or initiates reimbursements does NOT itself need an RBI Payment Aggregator / PPI license, as long as money never flows through the SaaS's own accounts (file hand-off to the customer's bank, connected banking on the customer's current account, or a regulated partner like RazorpayX/Cashfree initiates the payout).

- **Verdict:** confirmed  (confidence: high)
- **Corrected / best-supported:** Substantially CORRECT and well-aligned with RBI's framework. The decisive regulatory trigger under the RBI Payment Aggregator (PA) Directions is the HANDLING / RECEIVING / POOLING of other parties' funds in your own account. A SaaS that only generates payout files, formats instructions, or calls an API while funds move on the CUSTOMER's own current account (or are debited from a regulated partner's escrow/pooled account) is a technology provider, NOT a Payment Aggregator, and needs no PA authorization or PPI license. A license (and escrow/nodal) becomes mandatory the moment customer/payer funds land in, are pooled in, or are settled out of an account that the SaaS owns or controls. Two important refinements: (1) the PA framework is written around COLLECTION (customer-to-merchant), so even a pure payout/disbursement model that did hold funds sits in a regulatory grey zone rather than squarely inside the PA definition — but the safe rule is identical: don't hold third-party money. (2) The exemption holds only if the SaaS genuinely never has dominion over the money; nominal 'pass-through', 'sweep', or 'pre-funding to our account' structures defeat it.

## Reasoning

RBI's 2020 Guidelines on Regulation of Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways (effective 1 April 2020, consolidated into the 15 Sept 2025 Master Direction) draw the regulated/unregulated line precisely on fund handling. Verbatim: a Payment Aggregator is an entity that facilitates merchants to connect with acquirers and, in doing so, 'receive payments from customers, pool and then transfer them on to the merchants after a time period.' A Payment Gateway is an entity providing 'technology infrastructure to route and facilitate the processing of an online payment transaction without any involvement in handling of funds.' RBI itself states the distinction is fund handling: aggregators handle funds (require authorization under the Payment & Settlement Systems Act, 2007), gateways do not (no authorization; only voluntary baseline technology recommendations). This directly validates the claim's core logic: if money never sits in or flows through the SaaS's own accounts, the SaaS is a gateway/technology provider, not a PA.\n\nWHEN A LICENSE / NODAL / ESCROW IS TRIGGERED: The escrow obligation attaches specifically to non-bank PAs that 'maintain the amount collected by them in an escrow account with a single scheduled commercial bank.' The trigger is collecting/pooling funds. The instant payer or company funds are credited to an account the SaaS owns or controls before onward disbursement, the SaaS is pooling funds and falls into PA territory (PA authorization, ~Rs 15 cr / Rs 25 cr net-worth thresholds, escrow, audits, KYC). If instead the SaaS issues a prepaid wallet/card that it loads and from which it disburses, that is a separate PPI licence trigger (non-bank PPI: incorporated in India, Rs 5 cr min capital, Rs 1 cr+ net worth, own escrow) — again because the issuer holds customer money.\n\nCOMPLIANT ARCHITECTURES (all keep money off the SaaS balance sheet): (a) File hand-off / connected banking — SaaS generates the NEFT/RTGS/IMPS/UPI batch file or calls the customer's corporate-banking API; the debit happens on the CUSTOMER's own current account at their bank; SaaS never holds funds. Cleanest model. (b) Regulated partner initiates the payout — RazorpayX, Cashfree Payouts, Open, etc. The funds are debited from a current/escrow account held and controlled by the licensed partner (RazorpayX sits on a Razorpay-managed current account at partner banks like ICICI/Axis/RBL/YES); the SaaS is just an API caller/orchestrator. The regulatory burden sits with the partner, not the SaaS. (c) Pure SaaS-of-record where the company is the payer and SaaS only computes amounts and produces approvals/reports.\n\nGOTCHAS / SKEPTICAL CAVEATS: (1) 'Never touches your account' must be literal — escrow/nodal/pooled accounts in the SaaS's name, even held by a partner bank but controlled by the SaaS, count as handling funds and trigger licensing. (2) The PA Directions are collection-centric (customer-to-merchant); payout/disbursement-only providers are not the clean textbook PA case, so vendors that DO hold payout funds operate in a grey zone — RBI has been tightening payout/fund-flow oversight, so a hold-funds payout model is risky, not clearly exempt. Don't rely on 'payouts aren't collections' to justify holding money. (3) If using a partner, you ride on THEIR licence and KYC perimeter — the partner (not you) must KYC the end recipients; your contract should make clear you never have dominion over funds. (4) Cross-border reimbursements (paying overseas employees/vendors) bring FEMA in addition: purpose codes, AD bank involvement, possibly a PA-Cross Border licence for the partner — a different regime from domestic PA rules. (5) Closed-loop credits usable only within your own platform are not a 'payment system' and need no licence, but the moment value is cashable/transferable to third parties, PPI rules bite.\n\nNet: the claim's principle is correct and matches RBI's stated fund-handling test, with the single most important nuance being that the exemption survives only if the SaaS has zero dominion over the money at every step.
