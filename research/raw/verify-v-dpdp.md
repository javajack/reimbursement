# VERIFY: A SaaS storing employee/vendor PAN, bank details, Aadhaar, and salary/expense data must comply with India's DPDP Act 2023, with penalties up to Rs 250 crore for breaches — including obligations on consent, purpose limitation, data-fiduciary duties, breach notification, and penalties; plus enterprise buyer expectations of ISO 27001 / SOC 2 Type II / VAPT.

- **Verdict:** confirmed  (confidence: high)
- **Corrected / best-supported:** The claim is accurate and well-supported, with precision caveats. (1) Rs 250 crore is the STATUTORY MAXIMUM/ceiling per the DPDP Act Schedule, specifically for failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach (Sec 8(5)). It is not a typical or automatic fine; the Data Protection Board sets the actual amount based on nature, gravity, duration, volume/sensitivity of data, and harm. Different violations carry different caps: breach-notification failure and children-data violations up to Rs 200 crore, Significant Data Fiduciary failures up to Rs 150 crore, and a residual any-other-provision cap up to Rs 50 crore. (2) Such a SaaS is typically a Data Processor for its customers (and a Data Fiduciary for its own employee data). PAN/bank/salary/expense are personal data under DPDP, but the Act has NO special sensitive-data category and does NOT itself regulate Aadhaar (Aadhaar is governed by the Aadhaar Act 2016 / UIDAI). Obligations are real: consent (free, specific, informed, unconditional, affirmative), itemized notice, purpose limitation, retention limits/erasure, reasonable security safeguards (encryption, access controls, log retention), and breach notification. Timeline as of June 2026: DPDP Rules 2025 were notified in the Gazette on 14 Nov 2025 with phased/staggered enforcement — Data Protection Board and definitions live now; consent-manager rules ~12 months; full data-fiduciary obligations (notice, consent, rights, breach, retention, security) at ~18 months (approx May 2027). Breach notification requires intimating the Board without delay plus a detailed report within 72 hours, AND notifying affected individuals without delay (broader than GDPR — no risk-based de-minimis threshold). Enterprise buyer expectations are accurate: Indian enterprises (Tata, Reliance, banks) name ISO 27001 in vendor due diligence; US/global buyers prioritize SOC 2 Type II; VAPT/periodic security testing is expected (and effectively required for regulated sectors via RBI/SEBI/CERT-In, and to evidence reasonable security safeguards under DPDP).

## Reasoning

I verified the penalty Schedule directly: Rs 250 cr is the maximum for Sec 8(5) security-safeguard failures (the tier most tied to breaches), confirming the headline number is correct as a ceiling. I confirmed it is a cap, not a typical fine — the Board applies risk-based factors, and violations can stack across tiers. I confirmed the DPDP Rules 2025 were notified 14 Nov 2025 with a phased timeline (now / 12 mo / 18 mo approx May 2027), correcting any impression the law is fully enforceable today. I confirmed the substantive obligations (consent, notice, purpose limitation, retention/erasure, security safeguards, 72-hour-plus-without-delay breach notification to both Board and individuals). I flagged two overstatement risks the original claim glosses over: DPDP does not specifically regulate Aadhaar (that is the Aadhaar Act/UIDAI domain) and has no GDPR-style sensitive-data tier; and the SaaS is generally a Data Processor for customer data with fiduciary liability flowing primarily to the customer, though contractual/direct duties and fiduciary status for its own staff data still apply. Enterprise-buyer expectations (ISO 27001 / SOC 2 Type II / VAPT) are corroborated by multiple India-market sources. All elements are multiply sourced.
