# Market sizing for employee & vendor expense/spend-management SaaS in India (SMB-focused, indie-buildable), 2023-2026

## Executive Summary

India is a credible but crowded niche for SMB expense/spend-management SaaS. No tier-1 analyst publishes a standalone "India expense-management software market" number; analysts size the GLOBAL market (Mordor: expense-mgmt software ~USD 7.7-8.5B in 2025-26, ~9% CAGR; Travel & Expense software ~USD 4.49B in 2025, 17.3% CAGR) and call out Asia-Pacific (incl. India) as the fastest-growing region (~17-19% CAGR, SME segment ~23% CAGR). India-specific software figures circulating online are mostly vendor/marketing extrapolations and should be treated as low-confidence. Demand denominators are large and well-documented from primary government sources: ~1.53 crore active GST taxpayers (June 2025), ~7.83 crore Udyam-registered MSMEs (Feb 2026, but ~97% are micro/non-paying), ~18-20 lakh MCA-active companies, and ~6.9 crore EPFO-covered formal salaried workers. India's 2024 business travel (T&E) spend is ~USD 38.3B (GBTA/Visa), the world's 8th largest and growing ~15% in 2025 - the core "spend under management" that expense software digitizes. A defensible India SMB expense-software TAM is roughly USD 250-400M today (derived), with a serviceable (SAM) slice of USD 80-150M for cloud SMB tools, and a realistic 3-year indie SOM of USD 0.5-2M ARR (low single-digit-thousand paying SMB accounts at ~Rs120/user/mo blended). The market is well-funded and competitive: Zoho Expense (Rs79-149/user/mo), Fyle (~$6.99-11.99/user/mo), Happay (acquired by CRED at ~USD 180M in 2021; 6,000+ India customers, ~USD 1B annual spend) and Volopay (~USD 77M ARR claim, USD 33.5M raised) - plus SAP Concur at the enterprise top. A key structural risk: RBI's 2024 clampdown on card-based B2B/commercial payments via intermediaries disrupted the "corporate card + software" bundle several fintech-led players rely on. An indie team should compete on price, GST/India-compliance depth and a narrow vertical or vendor-reimbursement wedge rather than head-on with funded card-issuing platforms.

## Risks / Caveats

- The India-specific TAM/SAM/SOM figures are DERIVED bottom-up estimates by this study, not published analyst data - confidence is low and ranges wide; denominators are solid but the % assumptions (share of companies with T&E workflows, ARPU, employees/firm) are judgement calls.
- Vendor-reported metrics (Volopay ~$77M ARR via Latka, Happay's $1B spend) are self-reported/marketing and may be inflated or stale; treat as directional, not audited.
- Global analyst CAGRs (Mordor T&E 17-19%) come from a tier-2 firm behind a paywall; methodology and India sub-segmentation are not transparent - APAC growth may not translate 1:1 to India SMB software revenue.
- RBI's evolving stance on card-based B2B payments and KYC could further disrupt any card-linked monetization; payments regulation is a moving target for 2025-2026.
- Udyam's 7.83 crore and GST's 1.53 crore numbers vastly overstate the paying market - ~97% of MSMEs are micro and most GST registrants are tiny; using these as the addressable base would massively inflate TAM.
- Zoho's bundled pricing and free tier set a near-zero price floor for SMBs in India, structurally compressing ARPU and making indie unit economics fragile without a differentiated, defensible wedge.
- Some search results reference 2026 dates due to the simulated June-2026 search context; cross-check the latest GST/MSME/EPFO/MCA figures against primary government dashboards before final publication.

## Recommendations

- Treat the India SMB expense-software TAM as ~USD 250-400M (derived, low-confidence) and SAM ~USD 80-150M; do NOT cite any single 'India expense-management market USD X' figure from a vendor blog as analyst data - none exists from a tier-1 analyst.
- Anchor pricing at or below Zoho (Rs79-149/user/mo) for SMB; an indie cannot win a head-on price war with Zoho's bundled suite, so differentiate on deep India compliance (GST input-credit reconciliation, e-invoicing, statutory per-diem, TDS on vendor payments) and fast self-serve setup.
- Avoid building card-issuing/B2B-card-payment infrastructure as the core wedge - RBI's 2024 commercial-card clampdown makes that fragile and capital-intensive; stay software-only (receipts, approvals, policy, reimbursement payouts via UPI/bank, GST reconciliation) and integrate cards as optional partner feeds.
- Consider a vendor-reimbursement / SMB accounts-payable wedge (less saturated than employee T&E), or a single vertical (field-sales-heavy SMBs, agencies, NBFCs) to escape direct competition with Zoho/Fyle/Happay/Volopay.
- Model SOM conservatively at USD 0.5-2M ARR over 3 years (low single-digit-thousand SMB accounts); validate willingness-to-pay above Zoho with 15-20 design-partner SMBs before building, since the SMB band is low-ARPU and churn-prone.
- Present TAM/SAM/SOM as a sensitivity range with explicit assumptions (active-company count, % with T&E workflows, avg reimbursing employees, blended ARPU) rather than point estimates, and label every derived number as a calculation, not a sourced market figure.
