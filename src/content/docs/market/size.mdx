India expense software is a small but genuinely fast-growing niche, not the large market the vendor blogs imply. No tier-1 analyst publishes an India-only number, so any single "India expense-management market USD X" headline you see is a derivation or an outlier dressed as a fact. Our defensible read: India expense/T&E software is roughly **USD 120-350M today** (point ~USD 150-250M), growing **~14-18% CAGR**. The serviceable slice an indie can address is **~USD 80-150M**, and a realistic three-year indie capture is **USD 0.5-2M ARR**. <span class="pill pill--build">Build</span> the opportunity is real; <span class="pill pill--caution">Caution</span> the size is overstated everywhere you will look.

## What is actually published vs what is derived

Three sources of "market size" circulate, and they are not equal. Analysts size the global market and call APAC fastest-growing. Vendors quote India numbers that are SEO extrapolations or syndicated outliers. The honest India figure is our own derivation, cross-checked two ways. We label each below.

| Figure | Value | Provenance | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Global expense-management software | ~USD 8.33-8.48B (2025-26), ~8-10% CAGR | Analyst (Mordor; Fortune Business Insights) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| Global T&E software | ~USD 4.49B (2025) base; APAC ~19% CAGR | Analyst (Mordor T&E page; not in THESIS spine) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| APAC region CAGR (incl. India) | ~17.1% (broad EM); ~19% (T&E) | Analyst (Mordor) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| India business-travel (T&E) spend, 2024 | ~USD 38.3B, world's 8th largest, ~15% growth in 2025 | Analyst (GBTA/Visa; FCM) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| India expense-software, MRFR | ~USD 593M by 2025, 14.1% CAGR | Vendor-syndicated, **outlier** | <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> |
| India expense/T&E software (our number) | ~USD 120-350M, point ~150-250M, ~14-18% CAGR | Our synthesis + adversarial check | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |

<p class="source-note">Sources: Mordor Intelligence, Fortune Business Insights, GBTA/Visa, MRFR (analyst / vendor-syndicated). India software figure is our derivation, medium confidence.</p>

:::danger[No India-only analyst number exists]
Do not cite any single "India expense-management market USD X" headline as analyst data. None exists from a tier-1 firm. MRFR's ~USD 593M-by-2025 is an outlier: its own global figure that year (~USD 6,599M) would make India ~9% of the entire world market, implausible when India is ~2-4% of global enterprise-software spend. Treat it as a ceiling, not consensus.
:::

## Top-down vs bottoms-up

Two independent methods both land in the low hundreds of USD millions, which is why we trust the range and distrust the USD 593M headline. Note the global anchor itself: the world EM software market is only ~USD 8.5B, so a single-digit-percent India slice is structurally in the USD 150-300M band, not above it.

| Method | Inputs | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top-down (India share of global) | India ~2-4% of a ~USD 8.5B global EM base, plus ~USD 4.5B T&E base; India runs hot on growth | ~USD 120-300M |
| Bottoms-up (headcount x ACV) | ~50k-150k addressable companies x ~USD 1.3-2.5k ACV | ~USD 150-300M |
| Convergent defensible TAM | Both methods overlap | **~USD 150-300M (band 120-350M)** |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation from MCA active-company counts and Zoho/Fyle pricing (own derivation, low-medium confidence). Ranges are sensitivity, not point estimates.</p>

The bottoms-up math, stated so you can argue with it: roughly 50k-150k Indian companies have real travel or reimbursement workflows and a willingness to pay for SaaS. A 30-50 reimbursing-employee firm at Indian per-user pricing runs an annual contract value of about USD 1.3-2.5k. At 100k companies times ~USD 1.5k you get ~USD 150M; the bull case of 150k times ~USD 2k reaches ~USD 300M. Every percentage in that chain (share with T&E workflows, employees per firm, blended ARPU) is a judgement call, which is why this is our derivation, not a sourced figure.

## Demand denominators (large, and mostly not your paying base)

Government registries give clean, primary headcounts. The trap is treating any of them as the addressable market. Each overstates the paying base for a different reason, so we list the headline and the deflator together.

| Denominator | Count | Provenance | Why it overstates the paying base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active GST taxpayers | ~1.53 crore (Jun 2025) | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | Most are tiny or composition filers; ~14.8 lakh are composition (GST stats, govt), not SaaS buyers |
| Udyam-registered MSMEs | ~7.83 crore | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | ~97% are micro and will not pay for SaaS; only ~3% small/medium |
| MCA companies | ~2.8M registered / ~1.8-2.0M active | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | The realistic formal universe, but most still lack travel/reimbursement workflows |
| EPFO formal salaried | ~6.9 crore members | Govt <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | Only ~21.7% of all workers are regular-salaried (PLFS, govt); the user pool concentrates in formal firms |
| India T&E spend, 2024 | ~USD 38.3B | Analyst <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | This is spend under management, not software revenue; software captures a thin slice |

<p class="source-note">Sources: CAClubIndia/GST statistics, PIB (Udyam, EPFO), MCA/Business Standard, MoSPI PLFS, GBTA/Visa (govt / analyst). Denominators are high-confidence; their relevance to the paying base is low.</p>

:::caution[The denominator trap]
Using 7.83 crore MSMEs or 1.53 crore GST taxpayers as the addressable base would inflate TAM by an order of magnitude. The serviceable universe is the ~18-20 lakh MCA-active companies plus the ~3% non-micro MSMEs, and only a slice of those run reimbursement workflows worth paying to automate. Size from the company count, deflate hard, then cross-check against the global anchor.
:::

## The numbers we commit to

| Layer | Figure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM (India expense/T&E software) | ~USD 120-350M, point ~150-250M, ~14-18% CAGR | Top-down and bottoms-up converge |
| SAM (cloud SMB tools an indie can serve) | ~USD 80-150M | ~30-40% of TAM, excluding enterprise (Concur turf), micro, and card-dependent revenue |
| SOM (indie, 3-year) | USD 0.5-2M ARR | ~2,000-8,000 paying users at ~₹120/user/mo blended realised ARPU (between the ₹79 annual Standard and ₹149 annual Premium points after free-tier dilution; see [pricing](/business/pricing/)); low single-digit-thousand accounts; under ~1.5% of SAM |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation, anchored to Zoho/Fyle pricing (own derivation, low-medium confidence).</p>

The SAM carve-out matters because the enterprise band holds the majority of T&E software spend (~59% in 2025, Mordor T&E, analyst), which is Concur and the funded card players. The indie target is the underserved 10-200 employee middle: the fastest-growing segment but also the lowest-ARPU and highest-churn band. Zoho anchors that floor near zero (Free to 3 users, then ₹79-149/user/mo annual), which is exactly why size alone is not the case for building. See [pricing](/business/pricing/) for how we survive that floor and [the wedge](/market/whitespace/) for where software, not size, still decides the deal.

:::tip[Growth is the real signal, not absolute size]
"Fast-growing" is well supported (~14-18% CAGR, APAC running ~17-19%, GST e-invoicing pulling even small firms into digitized receipts). "Large" is not. Build for the growth and the compliance wedge, not for a headline TAM. The honest read sits behind the [final verdict](/business/verdict/): a conditional build on a small, fast, defensible niche.
:::

## How to use these figures

Present TAM, SAM, and SOM as a sensitivity range with the assumptions exposed, never as a single point dressed as analyst data. When an investor or a vendor quotes you an India number above ~USD 350M, ask for the method; it is almost always the MRFR outlier or an SEO extrapolation. Our floor (~USD 120M) and the convergent point (~USD 150-250M) are the numbers to defend, and the SOM (USD 0.5-2M ARR) is the only one that should drive the actual build plan.
