<span class="pill pill--build">Build</span> <span class="pill pill--caution">Conditional</span>

Build it, but only as a narrow wedge: a software-only, **Tally-native, WhatsApp-first reimbursement product for sub-200-employee Indian SMBs and field-heavy verticals**, with money movement partnered out and revenue earned on software seats, not card float. Do NOT build a horizontal Zaggle / Happay / Volopay clone, and do NOT build a card-issuing or float business. The card pool is closed to an indie team; the software pool is not.

## What the product is

One loop, run for both employees and vendors: capture, then extract, then decide, then approve, then pay, then post.

```text
capture (WhatsApp / mobile / email)
  -> OCR + GST field extraction (GSTIN, ITC eligibility, line items)
  -> configurable rule engine (allow / flag / warn / cap / block / route)
  -> maker-checker approval (rupee-tiered Delegation-of-Authority + SLA escalation)
  -> payout from a configurable source bank account (bank file hand-off or partner API)
  -> two-way accounting sync (Tally first, then Zoho Books)
```

The vendor-reimbursement side (TDS under 194C/194J, RCM, the MSME 45-day / Sec 43B(h) clock) is the adjacency that points at the larger, mandate-driven AP-automation market. Treat it as a deliberate path, not a feature checkbox.

## The verdict and why

Incumbents do not win on better software. Zaggle, the listed bellwether (FY25 revenue ~₹1,303 cr, +68% YoY), earns roughly 75 to 80 percent of revenue from card interchange and program fees; SaaS subscriptions are only about 3 percent of revenue (Zaggle 9MFY24 investor deck, vendor-stated, <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> confidence on the precise share). That float-and-interchange pool needs RBI licensing (non-bank PPI net worth ₹5 cr rising to ₹15 cr) or a bank partner. An indie team cannot match those unit economics and should not try.

So compete where software still decides the deal. The wedge is India-native compliance and integration depth, which is hard to copy precisely because it is unglamorous.

:::tip[The wedge]

Tally-native two-way sync is the single biggest deal-maker. Tally is ~70 to 80 percent of Indian SMB accounting (vendor-claimed, not independently audited). Its interface is painful (desktop, on-prem, XML-over-HTTP on port 9000), which is exactly why foreign cloud tools like Fyle/Sage skip it. Pair it with GST ITC capture, WhatsApp/field capture, and cash-advance settlement. See [the whitespace](/market/whitespace/).

:::

The price umbrella is real. Zoho anchors the floor (Free up to 3 users; Standard ₹99/user/mo, ₹79 annual; Premium ₹199/₹149; min 5 users, plus GST). Foreign tools price in USD: Fyle/Sage ~USD 11.99 to 14.99/user/mo, roughly 10x the India software point. Do not undercut Zoho on raw price; win on India reimbursement workflow.

## The market in one paragraph

Small but genuinely fast-growing, not large. No tier-1 analyst publishes an India-only expense-software number, so treat any single "India market USD X" headline as suspect. Our defensible synthesis: India expense/T&E software is ~USD 120 to 350M today (point ~USD 150 to 250M), growing ~14 to 18 percent CAGR [our synthesis + adversarial check, medium]. MRFR's ~USD 593M-by-2025 figure is an outlier ceiling, not consensus. A bottoms-up build (~50k to 150k addressable companies x ~USD 1.3 to 2.5k ACV) lands at ~USD 150 to 300M TAM, SAM ~USD 80 to 150M, with a realistic 3-year indie SOM of ~USD 0.5 to 2M ARR [our derivation, low-medium]. The global frame: expense-management software ~USD 8.33 to 8.48B (2025 to 26), a range spanning two analysts (Fortune Business Insights ~8.33B, Mordor ~8.48B), with APAC the fastest region ~17.1 percent CAGR [analyst, medium]. Full numbers and method live at [market size](/market/size/).

**IN (3 to 6 months, small team):** WhatsApp + mobile + email capture; LLM-vision OCR with GST field extraction; configurable rule engine (allow / flag / warn / cap / block / route) with line-item-then-claim rollup; maker-checker FSM with rupee-tiered DoA and SLA escalation; PAN + bank penny-drop whitelisting; line-item partial approval; GST ITC tagging; Tally + Zoho Books sync; payout via bank file hand-off (optional RazorpayX); immutable audit trail; per-active-submitter pricing with a small free tier.

## Three ways it fails

1. **You compete on cards or float.** Structurally unwinnable against ~75 to 80 percent interchange economics without RBI licensing or a bank partner. Stay a technology provider.
2. **You drift into low-margin services.** At the ₹99 to 199 point with heavy onboarding and Tally/OCR edge-case support, a 2-person team can be dragged into consulting. Cap implementation; monetize seats.
3. **You hit the trust wall undercapitalized.** ISO 27001 (~₹5 L), DPDP, and eventually SOC 2 plus VAPT gate the first mid-market deal. Sequence ISO 27001 first; defer SOC 2 until a US-HQ deal funds it.

:::danger[The product invariant that never moves]

Funds must NEVER touch an account the SaaS owns or controls. Use file hand-off to the customer's own bank, a regulated partner (RazorpayX/Cashfree) initiating from its own escrow, or connected banking on the customer's current account. Pooling funds triggers Payment Aggregator (net worth ₹15 cr at application) or PPI licensing. Get a written legal opinion before go-live. See [money movement](/compliance/money-movement/).

:::

## Who should build it

A small, finance-literate, India-based team that can stomach the Tally integration grind and sell founder-led through CA firms, Tally resellers, and Zoho/Tally marketplace listings. The lead ICP: finance-led companies, 50 to 500 employees, Tally/ERP-first, field-or-travel-heavy. The economic buyer is the CFO, Finance Controller, or owner-founder; the champion is the Finance/AP or HR-Ops person drowning in manual reconciliation. Not for a team that wants to issue cards, raise large, or build horizontal breadth. The full scored decision is at [the verdict](/business/verdict/).
