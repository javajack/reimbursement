A reimbursement product earns trust on the happy path and keeps it on the ugly ones: the advance that outlives its trip, the manager who resigned mid-approval, the thermal receipt longer than a phone frame, the credit balance that hits zero in close week. Phase 1 treats these as a finite, specified list, not future support tickets. Two schema decisions absorb most of the pain; the rest is the master table below, each row pointing at the page that owns the behavior. Vendor-side hard cases (TDS, RCM, the MSME 45-day clock) are deliberately absent: they live in [Phase 2](/phase-2/overview/).

## Two decisions do most of the work

First: model the expense at the LineItem, not the claim header. One hotel folio mixes lodging with minibar; one GST invoice mixes ITC-eligible services with blocked food lines under Sec 17(5). Each LineItem carries its own category, GST detail, and reimbursable marking, and lines individually end `approved`, `capped`, or `rejected`; a claim with any approved line proceeds with `approved_amount_paise ≤ claimed_amount_paise`. Partial approval, mixed bills, and partial coverage are not features bolted on later; they fall out of the schema.

Second: hard cases get their own records, never new claim states. Advances live on Advance, recoveries on a recovery record, metering on the CreditEntry ledger. Claim.status stays an eleven-state machine an approver can reason about (the transition tables live in [State machines](/spec/state-machines/)), and every odd path lands in an AuditEvent rather than a mystery.

:::tip[The unlock, carried from v1]
Line-item granularity is still the single highest-leverage schema decision. Each line carries its own reimbursable marking, category, GST fields, and outcome; that one choice unlocks partial approval, mixed-bill coverage, and clean ITC tagging. <span class="pill pill--high">High</span>
:::

## Advances: money before receipts

The gap between issuing an advance and seeing receipts is the field-sales black box. Phase 1 models expense advances only; salary advances belong to payroll and stay on a separate ledger (vendor guidance, medium). The Advance record (`adv_` IDs) runs its own lifecycle alongside claims:

<Steps>

1. Finance issues an Advance against a per-employee exposure cap; an advance-scope rule can `block` issuance above it.
2. The employee spends and captures receipts as usual, offline tolerated.
3. At approval, the claim nets against the outstanding Advance; only the delta above it becomes a PayoutInstruction.
4. Excess is returned to the company and recorded against the Advance (never as a negative payout); overspend pays out normally.
5. The Advance closes at zero outstanding; finance dashboards track aging of open advances.

</Steps>

## Duplicate or honest resubmission?

Detection runs in three layers. Layer one at capture: a perceptual hash over the corrected image catches re-uploads, including the two-device offline case. Layer two at submission: a date plus amount collision raises a dismissible warning with Keep both / Keep one verbs; silent auto-merge happens only inside tight tolerances (Fyle precedent: amount tolerance 0.0002, five-day date window). Layer three is the `DUPLICATE_SUSPECT` rule firing `flag` on the approver card and the finance queue. Publish the matching rule in help docs, Brex-style, so nobody is surprised by it.

The detector must distinguish fraud from persistence. A `sent_back` claim resubmitted is the same claim and the same credit: free, and never a duplicate. A rejected claim is terminal; when its receipt reappears on a new claim, the warning shows the prior claim and its status and offers resubmit-as-new with lineage attached, so the approver sees history instead of a fraud flag. `DUPLICATE_SUSPECT` fires hard only when the prior claim is active or paid. And because AI-generated receipts went from roughly zero to about 14% of fraudulent documents in about a year (AppZen, Sep 2025) <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span>, deterministic checks (GSTIN checksum, totals arithmetic) stay in front of every auto-accept.

## When the approver does not exist

Approval chains rot in real organisations. A vacant manager slot resolves to nobody: the claim routes to the company fallback approver and surfaces on the admin dashboard flagged `rule_gap`. A last-level approver leaving mid-flight cannot strand claims: chains snapshot at submission, deactivating a user triggers graph validation, and in-flight claims keep their snapshot until an admin uses the explicit re-route action. Delegation comes in three audited forms (time-bound out-of-office delegate without account access, standing delegate, admin-assigned), every decision attributed "as delegate of". Delegation resolves one hop and never cascades: if the delegate is also away, the ApprovalStep `escalated` path takes over toward the fallback. When the submitter is the approver, prevent-self-approval (ON by default) marks the step `skipped` and the claim skips up a level; the SAP KB 1944022 self-approval deadlock is the cautionary tale.

:::danger[Routing invariant]
A claim is never blocked at submission because routing failed. Worst case it routes to the admin queue flagged `rule_gap`, visible and actionable. Empty approver slots are an admin alert, not an employee error.
:::

## The month-end crunch

Claims, approvals, and payout runs all pile into close week, so the product ships sweep tooling rather than hoping: a pending-by-approver dashboard, one-click remind-all, and approve-by dates tied to the close calendar, on top of the standing cadence (48-hour SLA, 24 hours above the high-value threshold, at most two reminders per item, IST-aware quiet hours).

:::caution[The close-week collision]
Credit exhaustion clusters in exactly the same week, and claims in `awaiting_credits` are NOT in the approval flow. Forty claims sitting queued during close means finance misses the payout cut-off and the soft paywall becomes a finance incident. So exhaustion is loud by design: admin alerts at 50/80/95/100% burn, a forecast ("out in ~N days"), a banner on every queued claim, the one-tap "remind your admin" nudge, and top-ups that drain the queue FIFO instantly. Bill shock churns customers (73% of bill-shocked customers churn within 90 days; Recurly-cited, directional) <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span>; silent queues would too. Details in [Credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/).
:::

## Capture hard cases

- **Long thermal receipts.** A 30-line pharmacy receipt does not fit one frame, stitching is unreliable, and dewarping curled paper is research-grade. The honest answer is multi-shot: overlapping segments captured as pages of one Receipt in the multi-page tray, each passing its own `quality_gate`. Fair use applies: up to 5 images ride inside one credit, so a 7-image claim costs 2 credits, stated before submit.
- **Forwarded HEIC.** iOS transcodes camera-roll picks to JPEG on the way into the file input, but images that arrive by email-in, are AirDropped, or are shared in from the gallery or another app via the Web Share Target, or picked from the Files app, can arrive as true HEIC (practitioner-verified, high). The server sniffs magic bytes, decodes HEIC where it can, and otherwise rejects with a plain instruction ("please send as a photo"), identically on upload and email-in paths.
- **Screenshot of a photo.** Sometimes legitimate (an e-receipt screenshot), sometimes a re-captured altered bill. The client records capture source (camera vs library) before the EXIF-stripping re-encode at `compress`; the server adds heuristics such as absent camera metadata on a claimed camera capture. The outcome is a fraud signal on the decision card: `flag`, never `block`. <span class="pill pill--flag">Flag</span>
- **The 20 MB original.** Email-in attachments and library uploads can exceed OCR provider caps (Google Vision rejects files over 20 MB and recommends at least 1024×768; vendor docs, high). The camera path compresses client-side at `compress` (about 2048 px long edge, JPEG q≈0.8, typically 200-500 KB per page; our derivation). Oversized originals downscale server-side; the original stays attached as evidence for the 8-financial-year retention window and OCR runs on the normalized copy. iOS canvas area caps (16.78 megapixels before iOS 18) are why the client processes one page at a time at 12 MP or less.

## Credit hard cases

Submitting at zero balance is not an error. The claim lands in `awaiting_credits`, visible and ordered; uploads continue to be accepted with Receipt.status `queued`; intelligent processing pauses; the employee gets the nudge. Pre-submission OCR is free under the fair-use cap, `max(20, 3 × remaining credits)` scans per month, while the balance is positive; at zero balance it pauses entirely and receipts hold in `queued`.

Expiry during the wait is governed by three precedence rules: claims never expire, credits do; `plan_grant` credits die at period end with an `expiry` entry (no rollover) while `pack_purchase` credits live 12 months; and consumption always takes the expiring-soonest credit first. Two consequences worth stating plainly: queued claims consume nothing until the drain moment, and a `sent_back` resubmission re-enters `submitted` directly even at zero balance, because its credit was already spent.

| Date | Event | Ledger entries | Queue effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 28 Jun | Balance 0; 12 claims submitted in close week | none | 12 claims land in `awaiting_credits`, order preserved |
| 29 Jun | Admin buys the Starter pack (₹1,999 = 250 credits) | `pack_purchase` +250, 12-month validity | queue drains FIFO instantly; 12 `consumption` entries against the pack |
| 1 Jul | Monthly `plan_grant` renews | `plan_grant` + monthly allowance, expires 31 Jul | nothing queued; new submissions consume the grant first, since it expires soonest |
| 31 Jul | Period ends | `expiry` zeroes any grant remainder; pack balance untouched | none |

Cancellation while in `awaiting_credits` consumes nothing and refunds nothing; there is nothing to refund. Cancelled claims that already consumed a credit are not auto-refunded, but admins can compensate through an `adjustment` entry on the ledger.

## Done means

- Every edge case on this page resolves to a deterministic state outcome; none ends in "contact support".
- No claim is ever blocked at submission for routing or credit reasons; it lands in a visible queue (`awaiting_credits`, or the admin queue flagged `rule_gap`).
- A `sent_back` resubmission never triggers the duplicate warning and never consumes a second credit.
- Clawbacks reconcile to the paisa without mutating any closed claim.
- A 7-image claim shows "2 credits" before submit, not after.
- In close week, queue depth, burn forecast, and pending-by-approver are visible on one finance screen.

These behaviors harden into transition tables in [State machines](/spec/state-machines/) and failure drills in [Ops runbooks](/phase-1/ops-runbooks/).
