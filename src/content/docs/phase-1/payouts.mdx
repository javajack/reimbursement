Payouts keep the product's two core promises at once: the employee gets paid on a date they can see, and the money never passes through us. The compliant default is a bulk-payment file rendered in the customer's own bank format and executed inside their own net banking. A per-customer payout API adds one-click disbursement through a regulated partner; a payroll export covers companies that pay reimbursements with salary. Below: the three modes, the beneficiary-verification gate, source-account routing, the rails, and reconciliation back to `paid`.

## One invariant, three modes

Claims arrive `approved` from the approval engine. Batches start in the [finance console](/phase-1/web/finance-console/) batch builder: finance filters approved claims by department, branch, or date, reviews the total, and locks a PayoutBatch. Adding a claim to a `building` batch reserves it; the claim remains `approved`, and removing it while `building` simply releases the reservation. Claims move to `in_payout` when the batch is exported (file mode) or starts executing (API mode); after that point a claim returns to `approved` only by an explicit finance write-off of a `failed` or otherwise unresolved instruction, always audited with employee notification. There is no per-payout software fee: file generation and exports sit inside the submission credit, per [credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/); partner API fees pass through at cost.

:::danger[RBI invariant: funds never touch our accounts]

Every mode below debits an account the customer or a licensed partner controls, never one we own. The RBI Payment Aggregator definition covers customer-to-merchant collections: a non-bank PA needs ₹15 crore net worth at application, ₹25 crore by year three; a non-bank PPI issuer needs ₹5 crore rising to ₹15 crore (govt primary). <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> Any pooled, nodal, swept, or pre-funded account in our name re-characterises us as a PA or PPI issuer. The 2025 Directions offer no explicit payout-only safe harbour (the exemption is by exclusion), so a written legal opinion is mandatory before go-live: [money movement](/compliance/money-movement/).

:::

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Bank file (default)">

We render one bulk-payment file per source account in the customer's bank format; they execute it themselves. Per-payout cost on the file path is low and bank-schedule dependent: RBI waived its NEFT/RTGS processing charges and online NEFT is free for savings accounts, but corporate bulk-upload pricing varies by bank; re-verify per customer (govt + bank schedules, medium). It works for any company with corporate net banking: the entire target market.

<Steps>

1. **Build.** Finance locks the `building` batch; every claim gets a PayoutInstruction in `pending`.

2. **Export.** One file per source BankAccount in that bank's layout: ICICI CIB, HDFC ENet, Axis PayPro, SBI CINB, Kotak corporate net banking. Layouts are indicative until verified per bank; per-bank field maps and caps live in [bank files](/reference/bank-files/). Every row carries our `pin_` reference in the remarks field. Batch becomes `exported`, instructions `dispatched`, and member claims move to `in_payout`.

3. **Execute.** Finance uploads the file in their own net banking and authorizes it under the bank's own maker-checker. They mark the batch `executed` in Kharcha.

4. **Reconcile.** Finance imports the bank's return or UTR file (or pastes UTRs for small runs). Matched rows mark instructions `paid` and claims `paid`; reject rows mark instructions `failed` and enter the repair loop. The batch reaches `reconciled` only when no instruction is unresolved.

</Steps>

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payout API (per-customer opt-in)">

A RazorpayX or Cashfree-class regulated partner initiates NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, or UPI payouts from a customer-funded, partner-held account; we are the API caller, never the fund holder. The batch runs `building`, `executing`, `completed` or `partially_failed`, `reconciled`, with status webhooks and UTR on success (vendor docs, high). Per-transaction cost runs roughly ₹1.75 to ₹5 plus GST, passed through to the customer; RazorpayX advertises 250 free payouts a month and a ₹5 lakh single-payout cap. <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> (vendor pages, not rate cards; re-verify at build time). Every initiation POST carries an `Idempotency-Key`, so retries never double-pay. Opt-in is per customer; the default stays the file.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payroll export">

A CSV keyed by employee ID (name, total amount, claim references, period) that finance imports into their payroll tool. When the payroll run is confirmed, finance bulk-marks the claims paid-via-payroll with the payroll run reference as the audit anchor: claims go `paid` with `payment_mode: payroll` and no UTR. Tax posture matters: a receipted business-expense reimbursement is not salary and must not land in a taxable pay head; per-diems are exempt only to the extent actually spent (Sec 10(14)/Rule 2BB). Mapping rules live in the [regulatory map](/compliance/regulatory-map/). This is the slowest mode: monthly payroll cadence stretches time-to-money, and expected dates must say so honestly.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::caution[Bank formats are versioned and gated]

Field order, fixed-width positions, encryption, and per-file caps differ by bank and channel, and some specs publish only inside the corporate portal. Indicative caps: HDFC ENet near 1,000 transactions and ₹10 crore per file; SBI corporate RTGS/NEFT up to ₹50 lakh per transaction and ₹2 crore per day; Kotak's layout was not independently fetched. Confirm each format against the bank's current spec or the customer's relationship manager before building the renderer (primary/secondary mix; medium on exact fields).

:::

## The beneficiary gate

No PayoutInstruction is ever created against a BeneficiaryAccount that is not `verified`, with exactly one exception, the finance-acknowledged first-payout probation described below; the server enforces this in both modes that carry account numbers (payroll mode delegates account custody to the payroll system). Verification is penny-drop: deposit ₹1 through a verification API, read back the account-holder name as the bank holds it, and match it against the employee's registered name. The check costs about ₹3 per account (Setu, high). Any employee edit to account number or IFSC immediately demotes the account and re-runs the gate.

| Name-match result | Outcome | BeneficiaryAccount status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong match (exact, or trivial variation) | <span class="pill pill--allow">Allow</span> auto-verify | `verified` |
| Partial match (initials, order swap, honorifics) | <span class="pill pill--flag">Flag</span> finance review, both names shown | `pending` (finance review) |
| Mismatch, or penny-drop failure (invalid or closed account) | <span class="pill pill--block">Block</span> employee must fix details | `failed` |

For the rare customer that refuses every third-party API, the fallback is first-payout probation: the account stays `pending` under an explicit, audited finance acknowledgment, rides only in that single acknowledged batch, and flips to `verified` on its first successful UTR. Penny-drop remains the default; ₹3 once per employee is noise next to one mis-credited payout.

## Source-account routing

Real companies debit travel from one current account and everything else from another, and branch-heavy companies split by branch. BankAccount models the company source account; routing rules map a branch or category to a BankAccount, with the default account as fallback. The source account hangs off the batch, never the individual instruction: the bank-file path emits one file per source account per run, and the API path selects the partner fund account per batch. Spreading runs across banks also cuts single-bank downtime risk (EnKash and Open run exactly this pattern; vendor, high).

## The rails

NEFT is the default for employee reimbursements: no cap, batched, cheap. IMPS or UPI serve urgent small payouts on the API path; RTGS covers the rare claim of ₹2 lakh and above.

| Rail | Min | Max per txn | Speed | Indicative partner cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NEFT | None (₹1) | No maximum | Batched, 24x7 since 16 Dec 2019 | ~₹2-3 + GST |
| RTGS | ₹2 lakh | No maximum | Near-real-time, 24x7 since 14 Dec 2020 | ~₹1.75-5 + GST |
| IMPS | None | ₹5 lakh | Instant, 24x7 | ~₹3-5 + GST |
| UPI | None | ₹1 lakh standard | Instant, 24x7 | ~₹2-5 + GST |

<p class="source-note">Limits and timings: ClearTax and NPCI circular UPI-OC-185A (govt/analyst, high). The cost column applies only to the API path and comes from RazorpayX/Cashfree product pages, not contractual rate cards (vendor, medium); on the file path, per-payout cost follows the customer's bank schedule (RBI waived its own processing charges; corporate bulk-upload pricing varies by bank). Individual banks impose stricter sub-limits than NPCI/RBI ceilings; re-verify at build time.</p>

One nuance worth hard-coding: UPI's ₹5 lakh tier applies only to specific categories (insurance, capital markets, education, hospitals, government e-marketplace, IPO) under NPCI circular UPI-OC-185A. Never assume it for reimbursements: above ₹1 lakh route NEFT; at ₹2 lakh and above prefer RTGS.

## The reconciliation truth table

PayoutInstruction.status runs `pending` (batch building), `dispatched` (in an exported file or accepted by the partner), then `paid` or `failed`; `cancelled` covers instructions in a batch cancelled while `building`. Instructions are immutable attempts: repair creates a new instruction on the same claim, so the audit trail keeps every attempt. A `building` batch can also be `cancelled`, releasing every reservation; its claims never left `approved`. Formal transition tables with guards live in the spec's state machines.

| Batch status | Instruction status | Claim status | Who moves next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `building` | `pending` | `approved` (reserved) | Finance: lock, pull a claim (releases the reservation), or cancel the batch |
| `exported` | `dispatched` | `in_payout` | Finance: upload and authorize in net banking |
| `executed` | `dispatched` | `in_payout` | System: awaiting return file or UTR import |
| `executed` | `paid` | `paid` | System: matched on the stamped `pin_` reference; AuditEvent plus employee notification |
| `executed` | `failed` | `in_payout` (repair) | Finance: repair queue |
| `executing` (API) | `dispatched` | `in_payout` | System: awaiting partner webhook |
| `completed` (API) | `paid` | `paid` | System: UTR captured from webhook |
| `partially_failed` (API) | mixed | mixed | Finance: repair the failed instructions |
| `reconciled` | all terminal | `paid` | Nobody: closes only at zero unresolved instructions |

Matching is reference-first: the `pin_` id we stamped into the remarks field is the join key, with UTR stored on match. Return rows that fail reference matching fall into a manual-match queue keyed on amount, account, and a date window. Nothing flips to `paid` without a UTR, a payroll run reference, or an explicit finance confirmation.

## The repair loop

Return files and failure webhooks normalise to a small reason taxonomy: invalid account number, account closed or frozen, invalid IFSC (bank mergers rewrite IFSCs), beneficiary-name mismatch at the receiving bank, credit returned. The repair queue in the finance console shows reason, claim, employee, and one-click reissue. Reissue creates a new PayoutInstruction (new `pin_`) on the same claim in a new `building` batch, with an `instruction.reissued` AuditEvent linking old to new; account-level reasons first demote the BeneficiaryAccount to unverified, which notifies the employee to fix details and re-pass the gate. After three failed attempts the claim parks until the employee updates details. Throughout repair the claim holds at `in_payout`; finance returns it to `approved` only by an explicit write-off of its `failed` or otherwise unresolved instruction (audited, employee notified), say when a payout needs rethinking on a wrong source account. That is the same guard stated in the batch builder above: free removal exists only while the batch is `building`.

### Recovery settlement

Clawback after `paid` (an audit finding, a duplicate caught late) is a separate recovery record referencing the claim and its lines, never a new claim state: the claim stays `paid` and never reopens, per [edge cases](/phase-1/edge-cases/). The record settles by netting against the employee's next PayoutInstruction (the instruction's amount is the approved amount less any netting) or, where the company prefers, by emitting a payroll-deduction export line; finance can also mark it settled on out-of-band collection. The record shape lives in the [data model](/spec/data-model/).

## The payout calendar and expected dates

"When do I get paid" goes unanswered in-product category-wide: 43% of employees say slow reimbursement hurts their work experience, against an average claim-processing cycle near 9 days (Webexpenses UK 2024 survey; vendor, medium, directional). Our answer is a calendar the employee can see, not a vague status.

Companies configure cadence and cut-off per payout mode. The shipped default is `manual`: no calendar fires before a bank account exists. The recommended cadence once payouts are live is a weekly run on Friday with a Thursday 18:00 IST cut-off; twice-monthly and monthly presets exist, and payroll mode inherits the payroll date. Expected-date logic: a claim approved before a run's cut-off shows that run's date; show dates, never hours; if a run slips or a batch is skipped, recompute and notify with the new date. These dates power the claim timeline in [submit and track](/phase-1/capture/submit-and-track/).

| Moment | Employee sees |
| --- | --- |
| Approved before cut-off | "Approved. In the Fri 13 Jun payout run." |
| Batch `exported` or `executing` | "Payment is with Acme's bank." |
| Paid | "Paid 13 Jun. UTR ICICN26061012345. Banks can take a few hours to reflect it." |
| Instruction failed | "We could not pay your account ending 4321. Tap to check your bank details." |
| Payroll mode | "Will be paid with June payroll, expected 30 Jun." |

## Done means

- Finance can build, lock, and export a batch of approved claims; one file per source BankAccount in that account's channel format; regeneration is deterministic and idempotent.
- The server refuses any PayoutInstruction against a BeneficiaryAccount that is neither `verified` nor under finance-acknowledged first-payout probation, in every mode that carries account numbers.
- Return-file import auto-matches on the stamped `pin_` reference; unmatched rows queue for manual match; nothing becomes `paid` without UTR, payroll reference, or explicit finance confirmation.
- Every batch, instruction, and claim transition writes an AuditEvent; paid claims show their UTR to the employee.
- Failed instructions surface in a repair queue with one-click reissue as a new instruction on the same claim, capped at three attempts before parking.
- Approved claims display an expected payout date computed from the company calendar, and any date change notifies the employee.
