India expense software is a small but genuinely fast-growing niche: roughly **USD 120-350M today** (point USD 150-250M), growing **~14-18% CAGR** [our synthesis + adversarial check, medium]. That v1 sizing survives intact; it was adversarially verified and nothing in v2 changes the market. What changes is the cut. Revenue is no longer seats times ARPU: under metered pricing it is **paying companies × submissions × ₹ per credit**, and unlimited free seats let us sell beneath the 5-user minimum floor that gates Zoho's paid tiers. <span class="pill pill--build">Conditional Build</span>: the opportunity is real, but the size is overstated everywhere you will look.

## What is published vs what is derived

Three kinds of "market size" circulate, and they are not equal. Analysts size the global market and call APAC fastest-growing. Vendors quote India numbers that are SEO extrapolations or syndicated outliers. The honest India figure is our own derivation, cross-checked two ways. We label each.

| Figure | Value | Provenance | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Global expense-management software | ~USD 8.33-8.48B (2025-26), ~8-10% CAGR | Analyst (Mordor; Fortune Business Insights) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| Global T&E software | ~USD 4.49B (2025); APAC ~19% CAGR | Analyst (Mordor T&E) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| APAC region CAGR (incl. India) | ~17.1% (broad EM); ~19% (T&E) | Analyst (Mordor) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| India business-travel (T&E) spend, 2024 | ~USD 38.3B, world's 8th largest, ~15% growth in 2025 | Analyst (GBTA/Visa; FCM) | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| India expense-software, MRFR | ~USD 593M by 2025, 14.1% CAGR | Vendor-syndicated, **outlier** | <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> |
| India expense/T&E software (our number) | ~USD 120-350M, point ~150-250M, ~14-18% CAGR | Our synthesis + adversarial check | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |

<p class="source-note">Sources: Mordor Intelligence, Fortune Business Insights, GBTA/Visa, MRFR (analyst / vendor-syndicated). The India software figure is our derivation, medium confidence.</p>

:::danger[No India-only analyst number exists]
Do not cite any single "India expense-management market USD X" headline as analyst data. None exists from a tier-1 firm. MRFR's ~USD 593M-by-2025 is an outlier: its own global figure that year (~USD 6,599M) would make India ~9% of the entire world market, implausible when India is ~2-4% of global enterprise-software spend. Treat it as a ceiling, not consensus.
:::

## Top-down vs bottoms-up

Two independent methods land in the low hundreds of USD millions, which is why we trust the range and distrust the USD 593M headline. The global anchor itself constrains the answer: a ~USD 8.5B world market makes a single-digit-percent India slice structurally a USD 150-300M band.

| Method | Inputs | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top-down (India share of global) | India ~2-4% of a ~USD 8.5B global EM base, plus ~USD 4.5B T&E base; India runs hot on growth | ~USD 120-300M |
| Bottoms-up (headcount × ACV) | ~50k-150k addressable companies × ~USD 1.3-2.5k ACV | ~USD 65-375M raw; ~150-300M after excluding implausible corner combinations |
| Convergent defensible TAM | Both methods overlap | **~USD 150-300M (band 120-350M)** |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation from MCA active-company counts and Zoho/Fyle pricing (own derivation, low-medium confidence). Ranges are sensitivity, not point estimates.</p>

Note that the bottoms-up leg is priced in the incumbents' seat economics (a 30-50 reimbursing-employee firm at Indian per-user pricing runs ~USD 1.3-2.5k ACV), because TAM measures the revenue the market actually pays today. Our metered model competes for the same wallet with different unit math; it does not invent new TAM, it re-cuts who inside it can pay (see [pricing](/strategy/pricing/)).

## Demand denominators (large, and mostly not your paying base)

Government registries give clean, primary headcounts. The trap is treating any of them as the addressable market. Each overstates the paying base for a different reason, so we list the headline and the deflator together.

| Denominator | Count | Provenance | Why it overstates the paying base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Active GST taxpayers | ~1.53 crore (Jun 2025) | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | Most are tiny or composition filers; ~14.8 lakh are composition (GST stats, govt), not SaaS buyers |
| Udyam-registered MSMEs | ~7.83 crore | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | ~97% are micro and will not pay for SaaS; only ~3% small/medium |
| MCA companies | ~2.8M registered / ~1.8-2.0M active | Govt <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | The realistic formal universe, but most still lack travel/reimbursement workflows |
| EPFO formal salaried | ~6.9 crore members | Govt <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | Only ~21.7% of all workers are regular-salaried (PLFS, govt); the user pool concentrates in formal firms |
| India T&E spend, 2024 | ~USD 38.3B | Analyst <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | This is spend under management, not software revenue; software captures a thin slice |

<p class="source-note">Sources: CAClubIndia/GST statistics, PIB (Udyam, EPFO), MCA/Business Standard, MoSPI PLFS, GBTA/Visa (govt / analyst). Denominators are high-confidence; their relevance to the paying base is low.</p>

:::caution[The denominator trap]
Using 7.83 crore MSMEs or 1.53 crore GST taxpayers as the addressable base would inflate TAM by an order of magnitude. The serviceable universe is the ~18-20 lakh MCA-active companies plus the ~3% non-micro MSMEs, and only a slice of those run reimbursement workflows worth paying to automate. Size from the company count, deflate hard, then cross-check against the global anchor.
:::

## What unlimited seats does to the addressable base

The seat-priced market has a floor under it. Zoho Expense India: Free up to 3 users; Standard ₹99/user/mo (₹79 annual); Premium ₹199 (₹149 annual); **minimum 5 users**, plus GST [vendor page, high]. The cheapest paid entry is therefore about ₹495/mo regardless of how few claims a company actually files, and any company that wants approvers, finance, and an auditor in the system pays for each of them as a seat.

Our Free plan inverts that: unlimited users and approvers on every plan, 20 submissions per company per month included, with the meter on submissions (mechanics in [credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/)). Three consequences for sizing:

| Effect | Mechanism | Sizing impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base widens below the floor | Companies with 5-30 employees but thin claim volume are mispriced by seats; they enter free and pay only on volume | Larger entry funnel inside the same TAM; not new TAM in revenue terms |
| Break-even favors us at real usage | Break-even density vs Zoho ≈ 14 submissions/user/month, far above real usage [our derivation] | We undercut on price for nearly every realistic SMB |
| Revenue concentrates on volume | Field-heavy companies (sales teams, logistics, services) generate most submissions | SOM depends on submission volume, not headcount |

<p class="source-note">Source: Zoho vendor pricing (high confidence); break-even density is our derivation from the canonical pack ladder.</p>

This is deliberately honest framing: unlimited seats expands who can adopt, not how much India spends on expense software. The revenue case rests on converting volume, which is the wedge argument in [whitespace](/strategy/whitespace/) and the margin argument in [unit economics](/strategy/unit-economics/).

## SOM under metered pricing: the re-cut arithmetic

v1 modeled SOM as seats times ARPU. v2 revenue is:

```text
ARR = paying_companies × avg_submissions_per_company_per_month × effective_₹_per_credit × 12
```

The anchor unit, carried from the spine: a 25-employee company at an estimated 4 submissions/employee/month (our derivation; frequency unbenchmarked) files 100 submissions/month, about ₹700/mo at Growth-pack pricing (₹6,999 = 1,000 credits, ₹7.00 each), versus Zoho Standard at 25 × ₹99 = ₹2,475/mo (which still caps autoscans at 20/user) [our derivation from vendor pricing]. A 50-employee company at the same frequency runs ~200 submissions, about ₹1,400/mo. The full ladder (Top-up ₹9.98/credit down to Enterprise ₹5.00) lives in [pricing](/strategy/pricing/).

The three-year sensitivity, stated so you can argue with every input:

| Scenario | Paying companies | Avg submissions/co/mo | Effective ₹/credit | ARR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative (small-company blend) | 600 | 200 (≈ ₹1,400/mo each) | 7.00 | ₹1.0 crore ≈ USD 120k |
| Base | 1,500 | 300 | 6.50 | ₹3.5 crore ≈ USD 420k |
| Bull, volume path | 3,000 | 400 | 6.00 | ₹8.6 crore ≈ USD 1.0M |
| Bull, depth path | 600 | ~2,500 (field-heavy, ≈ ₹14,000/mo each) | 5.56 | ₹10.0 crore ≈ USD 1.2M |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation from the canonical pack ladder, at ~₹84/USD. Every row is a calculation, not a sourced market figure.</p>

Read the table honestly. Six hundred small accounts at ₹1,400/mo is only ~USD 120k ARR; metered pricing means lower revenue per small account than seat pricing, by design, because that is how we win accounts Zoho prices out. Reaching ~USD 1.2M ARR with 600 accounts requires a field-heavy blend averaging ~2,500 submissions/month (a 500-person field-sales company at 5 submissions/employee/month, on Scale-pack pricing). The realistic central case is **USD 0.4-1.2M ARR at year three**; v1's USD 0.5-2M band carries as the outer envelope, with the top end now explicitly requiring thousands of paying companies or a Scale/Enterprise-weighted mix.

:::caution[The frequency assumption is unbenchmarked]
Every row above multiplies through 2-6 submissions per employee per month, which is an estimate, not a benchmark. No published India figure exists for claim frequency by company type. Validate it with design partners before trusting any SOM row; it is the single highest-leverage unknown in this page.
:::

## The numbers we commit to

| Layer | Figure | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM (India expense/T&E software) | ~USD 120-350M, point ~150-250M, ~14-18% CAGR | Top-down and bottoms-up converge |
| SAM (cloud SMB tools an indie can serve) | ~USD 80-150M | ~30-40% of TAM, excluding enterprise (Concur turf), micro, and card-dependent revenue |
| SOM (indie, 3-year, metered) | USD 0.4-1.2M ARR central; USD 0.5-2M outer envelope | Companies × submissions × ₹/credit per the sensitivity table; frequency caveat applies |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation, anchored to analyst global figures and the canonical pack ladder (own derivation, low-medium confidence).</p>

The SAM carve-out matters because large enterprises hold the majority of T&E software spend (~59% in 2025, Mordor T&E, analyst), which is Concur and the funded card players ([competitors](/strategy/competitors/)). The indie target remains the underserved 10-200 employee middle: the fastest-growing segment, the lowest-ARPU band, and exactly where unlimited seats plus a submission meter changes the buying conversation.

:::tip[Growth is the signal; the wedge is the strategy]
"Fast-growing" is well supported (~14-18% CAGR, APAC running ~17-19%, GST e-invoicing pulling even small firms into digitized receipts). "Large" is not. We are not building a horizontal expense suite into a USD 150-250M pond against Zoho's bundle; we are building an employee-first capture-and-credits wedge whose economics work at small absolute scale. That stance carries from v1 and feeds the [verdict](/strategy/verdict/).
:::

When anyone quotes you an India number above ~USD 350M, ask for the method; it is almost always the MRFR outlier or an SEO extrapolation. Defend the floor (~USD 120M) and the convergent point (~USD 150-250M), and let only the SOM table drive the build plan.
