Unlimited users and approvers, free, forever; the meter is submissions per company. One credit equals one Claim submitted into the approval workflow, sold as prepaid Credit Packs with 12-month validity and a declining per-credit price, behind a permanent free tier of 20 submissions per company per month. This page is the pricing decision and its defense: the model, the ladder, the arithmetic against Zoho's seats, the exemplar evidence, the soft paywall that converts, and the risks carried plainly. The product mechanics (ledger, queue, alerts) are specified at [credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/); this page is why the model is shaped this way. <span class="pill pill--build">Conditional Build</span>

## Why the meter is submissions, not seats

Seats tax adoption. Every per-user rupee is a reason to leave the field team off the tool, and a half-onboarded expense product is worthless: the bills that never enter it are exactly the ones finance loses. Submissions track work. A company that files more claims gets more value, costs us more in OCR and storage, and pays more, in proportion. Value, cost, and price move together, which is the entire argument for usage-based pricing.

The market agrees, with caveats. OpenView's State of Usage-Based Pricing (2nd edition, Feb 2023) found 61% of surveyed SaaS companies had some usage-based component, and UBP companies reported median net revenue retention near 120% versus roughly 110% for subscription-only peers [analyst survey]. <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> Two honesty notes: OpenView wound down in December 2023, so the figures are directional and circulate via secondary syntheses; and the NRR gap is correlation, not attribution. Chargebee's 2025 report puts hybrid subscription-plus-usage adoption at 43% now [analyst]. Hybrids dominate pure pay-as-you-go, which shapes our launch sequencing below.

There is also a category-specific reason: pricing resentment is one of the six unresolved failure classes documented on [pain points](/strategy/pain-points/). Buyers already hate per-user fees stacked with scan meters (Expensify's 25 free SmartScans then $0.20 each; Zoho's 20 autoscans per user). Zoho's own autoscan cap is the tell: even inside seat pricing, the scan is the scarce unit. We make the real cost driver the explicit, honest price and give seats away.

:::note[The meter, precisely]

1 credit = 1 Claim submitted into the approval workflow. OCR at capture (up to 5 receipt images per claim), storage, approvals, payout-file generation, and exports are all inside that credit. Manual-entry submissions consume a credit too: the meter is the submission and its workflow, not the OCR. Resubmission after `sent_back` is free (same claim, same credit); failed OCR never consumes an extra credit. Beyond 5 images in one claim, +1 credit per additional 5: the anti-batching fair-use guard, stated plainly in the pricing FAQ.

:::

## The ladder

Prices are ex-GST, payable by UPI, netbanking, or card. Packs are prepaid, non-refundable, valid 12 months from purchase, drawn down FIFO oldest-expiring-first. The ladder declines roughly 2x top to bottom, matching the Clay and Postmark curves without their complexity.

| Tier | Price (ex-GST) | Credits | Effective ₹/credit | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free (plan, not a pack) | ₹0 | 20/month | ₹0 | Unlimited users and approvers; resets monthly, no rollover |
| Top-up | ₹499 | 50 | ₹9.98 | Impulse recharge; bridges to month reset |
| Starter | ₹1,999 | 250 | ₹8.00 | 1-3 months for the smallest buyers |
| Growth | ₹6,999 | 1,000 | ₹7.00 | The default recommendation |
| Scale | ₹24,999 | 4,500 | ₹5.56 | Mid-market quarters |
| Enterprise | ₹74,999 | 15,000 | ₹5.00 | Adds invoice billing and priority support |

<p class="source-note">Source: our derivation, anchored on verified COGS and the Clay/Postmark declining-curve precedent (THESIS-V2 canon). Per-pack sizing pending design-partner burn data.</p>

The free tier is deliberate, not generous: 20 submissions keeps a 5-person pilot free indefinitely while a 25-person company exhausts it in week one. Every pack card shows its effective per-submission price, the recharge-pack convention every Indian SMB owner already reads fluently.

## The arithmetic

COGS per submission is roughly ₹1.0-2.5: Google Document AI's expense parser at $0.01/page is about ₹0.85, an OCR-plus-LLM pipeline lands at ₹0.2-0.8, storage is under ₹0.05, compute and bandwidth ₹0.2-0.5 [vendor pricing plus our derivation]. <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> on the OCR rate, <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> on the all-in stack. That yields gross margin of 67-75% at ₹5/credit and 80-90% at ₹8-10/credit. The floor is ₹4/credit even in enterprise deals, preserving headroom for Phase 2 payout-API costs. The full cost stack lives on [unit economics](/strategy/unit-economics/).

Against the seat-priced anchor (full landscape on [competitors](/strategy/competitors/)):

| | Kharcha (Growth pack) | Zoho Expense Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25-employee company at an estimated 4 submissions/employee/month (our derivation; frequency unbenchmarked) | 100 credits/month ≈ ₹700/month | 25 × ₹99 = ₹2,475/month |
| Seats | Unlimited, free | Min 5 users, every user billed |
| Scan limits | None inside the credit | 20 autoscans per user per month |
| Idle users | Cost nothing | Cost ₹99 each |

<p class="source-note">Source: Zoho Expense India pricing page (vendor, high confidence); Kharcha figures our derivation from the canonical ladder.</p>

Break-even density against Zoho Standard is about 14 submissions per user per month: only above that does seat pricing become cheaper. Real usage sits far below it. But carry the caveat: the 2-6 submissions per employee per month estimate is unbenchmarked training knowledge with no published Indian figure behind it. Zoho's own 20-autoscan cap implies they expect well under 20, which is consistent, but pack sizing gets validated with design partners before launch, and that arithmetic is the spearhead of the sales narrative on [go-to-market](/strategy/gtm/).

## The evidence: what the exemplars teach

Every mechanic in this model has a working precedent. The table is the argument.

| Exemplar | What it proves | The lesson we take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DocuSign envelopes | Closest structural analogue: per-unit allowances with pay-as-you-go overage | Users batch documents into one envelope to dodge the meter; hence our 5-images-per-claim fair-use cap |
| Intercom Fin ($0.99/outcome, 50 included) | Charge-once fairness | One submission = one credit, regardless of OCR retries, send-backs, or approval steps; no charge on failure |
| Zapier held tasks | Queue-not-block | Over-limit work is held, never lost, replayable after top-up; silent holding angers users, so alerts are mandatory |
| Slack free history | Value withheld, access retained; every member feels it, only the owner can pay | Bottom-up upgrade pressure works; cite carefully: since Aug 26, 2024 Slack deletes free-plan data older than 1 year, so it is no longer pure withholding |
| OpenAI / Anthropic prepaid credits | Prepaid drawdown mechanics: minimum purchase, 12-month expiry, non-refundable, auto-recharge | Copy the mechanics; avoid the trap of auto-recharge firing unexpectedly (documented complaint threads) |
| HubSpot Credits | The anti-pattern | Monthly-expiring credits are the single most resented credit mechanic; our packs carry 12-month validity |
| Clay and Postmark | Declining marginal price curves (Clay ~$75 to ~$16 per 1k credits) | Bigger packs cost less per unit, rewarding prepayment without negotiation |
| WhatsApp Business API (India) | Indian SMBs already pay true per-unit software pricing in INR (utility ₹0.115-0.145/message since Jul 1, 2025, +18% GST) | Per-unit INR billing is normal here, not novel |
| Tally TSS | Perpetual license keeps working; the annual value layer lapses | Indian SMBs culturally accept "access retained, premium value paused", which is exactly our soft paywall |
| Telecom prepaid | Over 90% of Indian mobile users are prepaid in most circles | The recharge-pack-with-validity mental model is the native pricing UX for our buyer |

<p class="source-note">Source: vendor pricing and help pages, community threads, TRAI/PIB data, per research-usage-pricing.md and verify-v-pricing.md; confidence high on individual exemplar facts, medium on transferability.</p>

## The soft paywall is the conversion engine

At balance zero, nothing is lost and nothing is sold by ambush. Capture and upload continue; Receipts mark `queued`; submissions land in `awaiting_credits`; intelligent processing and workflow entry pause in a visible queue. The employee sees a calm banner ("Your bills are safe; processing resumes when credits are added") with a one-tap "remind your admin" nudge, the Notion-request and Figma-seat-request pattern applied to credits. The full state behavior is specified at [credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/) and surfaces in the employee flow at [submit and track](/phase-1/capture/submit-and-track/).

The loop that converts:

<Steps>

1. Admin alerts fire at 50, 80, 95, and 100% of burn, alongside a standing burn-rate forecast ("out in ~N days") on the admin home, via email, in-app, and a WhatsApp utility message.

2. Balance hits zero. Uploads keep landing as `queued`; submissions accumulate in `awaiting_credits` with queue depth visible to the admin.

3. Employees, who want their own money back, tap "remind your admin". The admin sees an aggregated nudge count next to a one-click buy path.

4. Top-up drains the queue FIFO, oldest first. Optional auto-top-up exists, default off, explicit opt-in with a monthly spend cap, an email receipt per top-up, and a one-click disable.

</Steps>

Prepaid drawdown is itself the bill-shock defense: spend is structurally capped at the purchased balance, no negative balance, no surprise invoice. The stat worth carrying, labelled: roughly 73% of customers who experience bill shock churn within 90 days [Recurly-cited via secondary sources, directional]. <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> precision, right direction.

:::caution[Month-end is the danger window]

Queued submissions are NOT in the approval flow. If credits exhaust during month-close, finance deadlines (and GST input-credit timing) meet a paused queue. Communicate loudly: queue-depth banners, forecast alerts well before zero, and explicit copy that `awaiting_credits` claims have not reached approvers. Handled quietly, this becomes a finance incident and a churn story.

:::

## Launch sequencing: packs first, hybrid later

Launch with the five-pack ladder only. No subscription billing on day one: the meter itself needs validation, and two billing systems double the surface area for accounting and support bugs. Once cohorts show stable burn, add the optional monthly auto-pack ("subscribe to Growth, +10% bonus credits"), which recreates the dominant hybrid subscription-plus-usage model (the 43% Chargebee figure, trending up) without ever having launched two systems. Bonus credits, not discounts, protect rate integrity; the ₹4/credit floor holds even in negotiated enterprise deals.

## Risks, carried plainly

| Risk | Mechanism | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Batching and gaming | Employees stuff many bills into one Claim (DocuSign envelope-stuffing precedent) | 5-images-per-claim fair-use cap, +1 credit per additional 5, published in the FAQ; never per-image pricing, which reintroduces unit anxiety |
| Expiry resentment | Non-refundable packs plus 12-month validity surprises first-time buyers | Expiry date on the balance widget, 30-day-before-expiry reminder, one-time goodwill extension policy |
| Month-end lockout | Exhaustion at close pauses the approval pipeline when finance needs it most | 50/80/95/100% alerts, burn forecast, instant FIFO drain on top-up, loud `awaiting_credits` messaging |
| Employee-vs-admin pain asymmetry | The paywall delays employees' own money; if admins ignore nudges, employees blame the product | Permanent 20/month free trickle, "your money is safe" copy, aggregated nudge counts that make admin inaction visible |
| Manual-entry free-riding | If manual entry bypassed the meter, companies could run manual-only forever | Closed by design: manual submissions consume credits; the meter is the submission, not the OCR |
| Deferred-revenue accounting | Prepaid credits create deferred revenue and breakage; 12-month expiry plus GST invoicing has tax consequences | Needs CA and auditor signoff before launch; flagged, not solved, in this dossier |
| Frequency estimate wrong | If real medians are 1-2 submissions/employee/month, the free tier is too generous for sub-10-person companies | Validate with design partners; free-tier size is a tunable, the model is not |

## GST presentation

All prices are quoted ex-GST with 18% added at invoice, the convention Zoho, Tally, and WhatsApp BSPs have already normalized for this buyer. Every pack purchase issues a proper GST invoice; for GST-registered buyers the 18% is reclaimable as input tax credit, so the sticker comparison against Zoho (also quoted ex-GST) stays apples to apples. Show the GST line explicitly on the pack card and at checkout; hiding it until payment is the kind of billing trap this category is resented for.
