Yes, but only with a deliberately tight free tier and a single, obvious paid trigger. <span class="pill pill--build">Build</span> A generous free plan is a slow leak: every free user who scans receipts and triggers payouts carries real per-transaction cost, and at India ACVs you cannot subsidise that at scale. Cap users, cap OCR scans, gate payouts, and treat the free tier as a funnel, not a product.

The model is proven in India. Zoho runs a free tier (up to 3 users) under a low-price paid ladder and won the category against far better-funded global incumbents. Copy the structure, not the generosity.

## Why freemium works here at all

Two conditions have to hold for freemium to pay off, and both do in this market.

First, the variable cost per free user must be small enough to absorb. It is, if you constrain the right features. OCR via LLM-vision runs roughly USD 0.0005-0.003 per document (Gemini Flash-Lite, Claude Haiku), or USD 8-10 per 1,000 docs on a dedicated parser like Textract AnalyzeExpense. A capped free user scanning a few dozen receipts a month costs cents, not rupees.

Second, the paid trigger must be both valuable and unavoidable. In a reimbursement product the natural trigger is money movement: the moment a customer wants to actually pay employees or vendors, not just track claims. That is also the most cost-bearing feature, which is exactly why it belongs behind the paywall.

:::tip[The design principle]
Put the cheap, sticky features (capture, OCR up to a cap, basic approvals) in free. Put the expensive, high-value features (payouts, multi-stage approvals, accounting sync, GST and policy reports) in paid. The free tier should make the paid trigger feel inevitable.
:::

## What the free tier should and should not include

Mirror Zoho's structure, then gate on India-specific value rather than racing it to the bottom on price. See [/business/pricing/](/business/pricing/) for the full ladder.

| Capability | Free | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Users | 3-5 cap | per active submitter |
| Receipt capture (WhatsApp/mobile/email) | Yes | Yes |
| OCR autoscans | Capped (~20-50/mo) | Higher / metered |
| Approvals | Single-stage | Multi-stage maker-checker |
| Payouts (bank file / RazorpayX) | No | Yes |
| Tally / Zoho Books sync | No | Yes |
| GST ITC and policy reports | No | Yes |

The OCR cap is the load-bearing limit. Set it generously and a free user filing for a busy field team can quietly consume hundreds of scans a month. Set it at a tight ceiling (Zoho's free plan allows 20 autoscans) and the cost is bounded by construction.

## The COGS trap

This is the part most indie founders underestimate. On a free tier, you eat every per-transaction cost yourself, and three of them are real money.

| Variable cost | Indicative rate | Source label |
|---|---|---|
| OCR (LLM-vision) | USD 0.0005-0.003/doc | vendor pricing, medium |
| OCR (dedicated parser) | USD 8-10 / 1,000 docs | vendor pricing, medium |
| Bank payout | ~₹1.75-5 per transaction (RazorpayX 250 free/mo) | indicative |
| Bank penny-drop verification | ~₹3 per successful, +18% GST | Setu published, high |

<p class="source-note">Source: Gemini / Claude API pricing for the LLM-vision OCR row; AWS Textract pricing for the dedicated-parser row; RazorpayX billing docs; Setu (vendor/primary, medium confidence). Figures are per-unit and scale with usage.</p>

The danger is not the headline rate; it is the multiplication. A single enthusiastic free user who onboards a 10-person team, scans 200 receipts, penny-drops 10 bank accounts, and pushes 50 payouts has cost you real rupees while paying nothing. Multiply across a popular free tier and the OCR/payout line item stops being a rounding error.

:::danger[Hard rule]
Payouts never ship on the free tier. Payouts and penny-drop verification are the costliest per-transaction features and the clearest paid trigger. Keeping them paid solves the COGS problem and the conversion problem at once.
:::

The defence is simple: model worst-case free-user behaviour before launch, not average. Assume the loudest free user, not the median one, and confirm the cost still rounds to noise at your free-tier caps. Then instrument actual usage and tighten caps if reality exceeds the model. The full cost stack and margin math live in [/business/unit-economics/](/business/unit-economics/).

## How to convert without a sales team

At India SMB ACVs you cannot afford an outbound motion to chase free users (outbound/SDR CAC runs ₹2-4 lakh, unaffordable here). Conversion has to be product-led.

<Steps>

1. Cap the free tier so a real team hits a wall (user count or OCR scans) within weeks, not years.

2. Surface the paid trigger in-product: when a free user tries to pay someone, that is the upgrade prompt.

3. Nudge in-app, not via a rep: show the GST ITC or faster-payout value the free tier withholds.

4. Use the CA, Tally-partner, and content/SEO channels (the lowest-CAC routes) to bring qualified free users in, then let the product close them. See [/business/gtm/](/business/gtm/).

</Steps>

## Bottom line

Freemium is viable and recommended, but it is a constrained instrument, not a growth hack. Win the category the way Zoho did: a small, genuinely free tier that demonstrates value, a paid trigger built around money movement and India compliance, and disciplined caps that keep per-transaction COGS bounded. Plan for 2-5% conversion, instrument it from day one, and never let payouts or unmetered OCR live on the free plan.
