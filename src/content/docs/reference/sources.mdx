Read every number in this dossier with its label attached. Most figures here are defensible; a meaningful minority are vendor-marketing stats reproduced because no better source exists, and a few are our own derivations. This page explains how the research was done, what the confidence pills mean, where the evidence is thin, and then lists the full bibliography grouped by topic. If a figure is about to drive code or an investment decision, re-verify it against the primary statute or filing first.

## How this was produced

The dossier was built by a multi-agent research pass: 14 domain researchers (one per area, from market sizing through OCR, payouts, KYC, and the regulatory map) plus 7 adversarial fact-checkers tasked with breaking the headline claims (market size, GST Section 17(5), the RBI licensing position, DPDP, KYC pricing, competitor status, and Tally dominance). Each researcher ran live web research, cited its sources inline, and the findings were then synthesized into [the thesis](/overview/executive-summary/) that every page must agree with. Those seven fact-checks map one-to-one onto the seven "Adversarial verification" bibliography blocks below (market size, GST Section 17(5), RBI licensing, DPDP, KYC pricing, competitor status, and Tally dominance), so the 14-plus-7 count is traceable on this page.

The verification pass mattered. It is the reason this dossier says "conditional build" rather than "build," corrects stale competitor facts (Happay sold on from CRED to MakeMyTrip; Fyle is now Sage; Sodexo benefits is now Pluxee), and refuses to repeat the implausible Volopay "USD 77.3M ARR" self-report as fact (the getlatka Volopay profiles in the bibliography below are cited only to flag the figure, not to trust it).

## Confidence conventions

Every load-bearing number carries a provenance label and, when soft, a confidence pill. The three provenance classes are deliberately kept distinct so a soft number is never laundered into a hard one.

| Provenance | What it means | Treat as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analyst | Tier-1 or tier-2 research house (Mordor, Fortune Business Insights, GBTA/Visa, MRFR) | Directional, check the year and CAGR |
| Govt / primary | Statute, regulator, or official registry (CBIC, RBI, MCA, PIB, MeitY, NPCI) | Authoritative for what it actually states |
| Vendor | Vendor pricing page, marketing blog, or self-reported metric | Lowest trust; corroborate before citing |
| Our derivation | Bottoms-up math or synthesis built in this dossier | Our opinion, shown with assumptions |

Confidence pills sit alongside: <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> for primary or well-corroborated facts, <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> for analyst ranges and triangulated figures, <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> for single-source or vendor-dramatized stats.

:::note[The split, in practice]

A vendor page can still be high confidence for its own pricing (Zoho Expense lists ₹99/user/mo, that is a fact about Zoho). The same vendor's blog claiming "60% ITC leakage" is low confidence, because the vendor has an incentive to dramatize the pain it sells against. Same source type, different trust, depending on what is being claimed.

:::

## Known limits

These caveats apply to the whole dossier. Carry them when you quote it.

- **No tier-1 India-only market number exists.** No major analyst publishes an India-only expense-software market size. Our ~USD 120 to 350M figure (point ~USD 150 to 250M) is a synthesis plus adversarial check, not a published consensus. MRFR's ~USD 593M-by-2025 is an outlier ceiling. See [market size](/market/size/) for the full reasoning. <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span>
- **Many pain stats are vendor-sourced.** The 40 to 60 hours/month finance load, 60% ITC leakage, 30 to 45 day cycles, and 18% ITC recovery figures come from vendor and marketing blogs with an incentive to dramatize. They are marked medium or low and labelled as such on [pain points](/market/pain-points/). Recommend primary interviews before betting on them. <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span>
- **Employee-voice evidence is under-sampled.** Reddit, Quora, and G2 primary threads were largely un-fetchable during research, so the lived-experience signal is thinner than we would like. Glassdoor returned a 403 to the crawler. Treat employee-pain claims as directional and validate with interviews.
- **Some primary statute pages were intermittently un-fetchable.** CBIC and Income-Tax department pages timed out or threw TLS errors at fetch time (the CBIC Section 17 page failed on 2026-06-09). Statute wording was corroborated via ClearTax, Taxmann, and IndianKanoon and should be re-checked against the live statute before being hard-coded into a rule engine. See [the regulatory map](/compliance/regulatory-map/).
- **Tally's ~70 to 80% SMB share is vendor-claimed,** republished from Gartner/McKinsey figures via secondary sources, not independently audited by us. Present it as such.
- **Rates and thresholds are time-sensitive.** TDS rates, e-invoicing thresholds, PA net-worth requirements, and DPDP phase-in dates change by financial year. Every such figure should be version-dated before it drives behaviour.

:::caution[Before this drives code or money]

Re-verify GST Section 17(5) clause wording, TDS rates by financial year, RBI Payment Aggregator net-worth thresholds, and DPDP obligations against the primary statute or the live regulator page. Get a written legal opinion before go-live on the money-movement architecture. The dossier is a build memo, not legal advice.

:::
