Read every number in this dossier with its label attached. The evidence base was built in two passes: a v1 feasibility pass (14 domain studies plus 7 adversarial fact-checks) that produced the conditional-build verdict, and a v2 build-deep pass (10 fresh studies plus 3 adversarial checks, June 2026) that produced the Phase 1 product canon. This page explains how both passes were run, what the confidence pills oblige you to do, where the evidence is thin, and then lists the complete grouped bibliography from both passes. If a figure is about to drive code or an investment decision, re-verify it against the primary source first.

## How both passes were run

The dossier rests on two multi-agent research passes. The v1 pass asked "is this worth building, and how": one researcher per domain, from market sizing through OCR, payouts, KYC, and the regulatory map, each running live web research with inline citations. The v2 pass asked "exactly how do we build Phase 1": fresh studies on the product mechanics the v1 pass had only sketched.

| Pass | Scope | Domain studies | Adversarial checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v1, feasibility | Market, competitors, regulation, money rails | 14: India incumbents; global leaders; market sizing; pain points; regulatory and tax; rule engines; OCR and document AI; KYC and onboarding; payouts and disbursement; integrations; pricing and GTM; personas; build feasibility; edge cases | 7: market size; GST Section 17(5); RBI licensing; DPDP; KYC pricing; competitor status; Tally dominance |
| v2, build-deep (June 2026) | Phase 1 product mechanics | 10, grouped under 7 bibliography headings: software pain points; mobile capture tech; OCR-itemisation UX; usage pricing; onboarding and configuration; approver UX; PWA-in-India | 3: camera and capture claims; scanning libraries and preprocessing gains; the pricing ladder and survey claims |

Each study in both passes followed the same pipeline:

<Steps>

1. **Live web research.** Every study ran against the open web with sources captured at fetch time. Bot-blocked pages were flagged as such and quoted only via search snippets, never silently presented as fetched.

2. **Structured findings.** Each study produced a findings file with every claim tied to a source, carrying provenance and confidence per claim. These files are the raw and raw2 research corpus behind the bibliography below.

3. **Lead synthesis.** Findings were synthesized into the thesis that every page of this dossier must agree with. Where a finding and the thesis conflict, the thesis wins, because it already absorbed the adversarial corrections.

4. **Adversarial review.** Dedicated fact-checkers attacked the headline claims. In v2 this extended to reviewing drafted pages against the research before publication.

</Steps>

### What the adversarial passes changed

The v1 verification pass is the reason this dossier says conditional build rather than build. It corrected stale competitor facts (Happay sold on from CRED to MakeMyTrip; Fyle is now Sage Expense Management; Sodexo benefits is now Pluxee) and refused to repeat the implausible Volopay "USD 77.3M ARR" self-report as fact; the getlatka profile is cited below only to flag that figure, not to trust it.

The v2 verification pass did the same for the build canon. It pinned the camera facts the capture spec depends on: stream frames are never distorted, so a stretched bill is always a presentation or capture bug; ImageCapture reached Safari only with iOS 18.4 (March 2025); WebKit bug 252465 still reproduces through iOS 18.4.1, which is why [the camera spec](/phase-1/capture/camera/) defaults to file-input capture in iOS standalone. It downgraded the OpenView usage-pricing survey to directional (the firm wound down in December 2023), flagged the Slack free-tier citation trap (Slack deletes data older than a year since 26 August 2024, so "value withheld, never deleted" must be cited carefully), and stress-tested the credit ladder against COGS (floor ₹4/credit, our derivation) before [pricing](/strategy/pricing/) committed to the Free, Starter, Growth, Scale plans. The queue-not-block precedents it verified (Zapier held tasks, Zoho's plan-limit-exceeded filter) are what justify the `awaiting_credits` soft paywall rather than a hard block.

## Confidence conventions

Every load-bearing number carries a provenance label and, when soft, a confidence pill. The five provenance classes are kept distinct so a soft number is never laundered into a hard one.

| Provenance | What it covers | Typical confidence | What it obliges you to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Analyst | Research houses (Mordor, Fortune Business Insights, GBTA/Visa, MRFR), business press, survey firms | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | Treat as directional. Check the publication year and the CAGR base year; never average across houses with different market definitions. |
| Vendor | Pricing pages, help docs, marketing blogs, self-reported metrics | <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> for its own prices and documented behavior; <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> for the pain stats it sells against | Trust a vendor about its own product. Corroborate anything the vendor has an incentive to dramatize before repeating it. |
| Govt | Statute, regulator circulars, official registries (CBIC, RBI, MCA, PIB, MeitY, NPCI, UIDAI) | <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | Authoritative for what the text actually states. Check the financial year and amendment history before relying on any rate or threshold. |
| Primary docs | Specs and standards, browser bug trackers, official developer docs, fetched GitHub issues, exchange filings | <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> at fetch date | Re-test at build time. Browsers ship monthly; a WebKit bug that reproduced in June 2026 may be fixed, or regressed, by your release. |
| Our derivation | Bottoms-up math and synthesis built in this dossier | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> at best | Read the assumptions, not just the conclusion. Every derivation states what flips it; if an input moves, re-run the math. |

Confidence pills sit alongside provenance on every soft figure:

| Pill | When it is used | What it obliges you to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> | Primary sources or multiple independent corroborations | Safe to build on. Still version-date regulatory figures by financial year. |
| <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> | Analyst ranges, triangulated estimates, single solid sources | Fine for direction and sizing. Do not hard-code; re-verify before code or money depends on it. |
| <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> | Single-source, vendor-dramatized, or snippet-mediated claims | Treat as a hypothesis. Validate with design partners or primary interviews before acting on it. |

:::note[The split, in practice]

A vendor page can still be high confidence for its own pricing: Zoho Expense lists ₹99/user/month, and that is a fact about Zoho. The same vendor ecosystem's claim that "up to 60% of T&E ITC is lost" is low confidence, because the claimant sells the cure. Same source type, different trust, depending on what is being claimed.

:::

## Known limits

These caveats apply to the whole dossier. Carry them when you quote it.

- **Review sites were bot-blocked.** G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, JustUseApp, and the Concur community returned 403s to the crawler, so the complaint quotes behind [pain points](/strategy/pain-points/) are search-snippet-mediated, with paraphrase risk. Archive a page before quoting it verbatim anywhere public. <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span>
- **Reddit was largely un-minable**, so employee-voice evidence is under-sampled. Complaint mining also over-samples angry users, and incumbents are actively fixing the bugs we cite. Validate pain claims in design-partner interviews before betting on them.
- **Vendor latency and accuracy claims are marketing.** Klippa's 0.5-4s extraction, Fyle's "seconds", Happay's 92% OCR accuracy: useful as the bar vendors set for themselves, never as benchmarks. Our own OCR targets are stated independently with their own provenance. <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span>
- **The cost baseline is 2015-vintage.** GBTA's ~$58 and 20 minutes to process a report, 19% error rate, plus $52 and 18 minutes per fix is still industry-cited a decade on. We quote it labelled, never as current fact.
- **Prices move.** WhatsApp/Meta per-message rates, competitor plans, OCR API prices, and payout fees change frequently. Nothing in this dossier is a quote; re-verify every price at build time.
- **The submission-frequency estimate is unbenchmarked.** The 2-6 submissions per employee per month figure drives the sales arithmetic in [pricing](/strategy/pricing/) and [unit economics](/strategy/unit-economics/). It is our estimate, to be validated with design partners before the ladder is treated as proven. <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span>
- **Statute wording came via secondary sources.** The CBIC Section 17 page threw a TLS error at fetch time (2026-06-09); GST and Income Tax wording was corroborated via ClearTax, Taxmann, and IndianKanoon. Re-check the live statute before hard-coding anything into the rule engine; see [the regulatory map](/compliance/regulatory-map/).
- **Prepaid-credit accounting needs a professional.** Revenue recognition for credit packs (deferred revenue, breakage, GST invoicing on prepayment) needs CA signoff before launch; this dossier flags the questions, it does not answer them.

:::caution[Before this drives code or money]

Re-verify GST Section 17(5) clause wording, TDS rates by financial year, RBI Payment Aggregator thresholds, DPDP obligations, and every browser-capability claim against the primary source. Get a written legal opinion on the money-movement architecture before go-live. The dossier is a build memo, not legal advice.

:::
