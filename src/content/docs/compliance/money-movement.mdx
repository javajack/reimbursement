Kharcha computes who is owed what; the money itself moves on rails we never touch. The default payout is a bank bulk-payment file the customer executes in their own net-banking, the optional per-customer payout API runs on a licensed partner's accounts and licence, and the payroll export leaves payment to the system that already pays salaries. Because funds never land in an account we own or control, RBI's framework classes us as a technology provider: no Payment Aggregator (PA) authorization, no PPI licence. <span class="pill pill--build">Conditional Build</span> The conclusion was adversarially verified in v1 against the 2020 guidelines and the September 2025 Master Direction. This page is where the [regulatory map](/compliance/regulatory-map/) becomes an architecture rule; [Payouts](/phase-1/payouts/) is where it becomes product.

## The line is fund handling, not money movement

RBI draws the regulated boundary on who custodies funds, not on who computes amounts. The 2020 Guidelines on Regulation of Payment Aggregators and Payment Gateways define a PA as an entity that receives payments from customers, pools them, and transfers them to merchants after a time period; a Payment Gateway provides "technology infrastructure to route and facilitate the processing of an online payment transaction without any involvement in handling of funds". Handling funds requires authorization under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. Pure technology does not; gateways get only voluntary baseline recommendations (RBI primary, high confidence).

The 2025 consolidation keeps the same test. The Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025 (15 September 2025, RBI/DPSS/2025-26/141) define a PA around "aggregation of payments made by customers to the merchants" and subsequent settlement of the collected funds to those merchants. That is a collections definition. An outbound reimbursement flow that only generates files or calls APIs against someone else's account is outside it twice over: we neither collect nor settle. The exclusion is definitional, not a granted exemption, which is why the legal opinion at the end of this page is gating, not ceremonial.

| Trigger | Regime | The bar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receive, pool, or settle third-party funds | Payment Aggregator (authorization under the PSS Act, 2007) | Net worth ₹15 crore at application, ₹25 crore by end of the third financial year; mandatory escrow with one scheduled commercial bank |
| Issue stored value (a wallet or prepaid instrument) you load and disburse from | Non-bank PPI | Net worth ₹5 crore at application rising to ₹15 crore by the third financial year; own escrow |

<p class="source-note">Source: RBI PA Directions 2025 and RBI PPI FAQ, read through AuthBridge and Trilegal summaries (govt primary plus analyst, high confidence).</p>

These bars are why the thesis non-goals (no cards, no prepaid instruments, no float, no holding funds) are legal load-bearing walls, not product taste. ₹15 crore at application is a payments company's balance sheet. The compliant indie path is to design so the trigger can never fire.

## Three payout modes against one test

All three Phase 1 payout modes were designed backwards from the fund-handling test. In every mode the BeneficiaryAccount an employee is paid into is verification-gated in-product (penny-drop, about ₹3 per check; Setu, high confidence) before it can enter a batch, and reconciliation is UTR-based. Mechanics and UX live in [Payouts](/phase-1/payouts/); per-bank field formats in [bank file formats](/reference/bank-files/).

| Mode | Funds debit from | Licence in the loop | Our role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bank bulk-payment file (default) | The customer's own current account (a BankAccount record) | The customer's bank | File generation only |
| Payout API (optional, per customer) | An account the licensed partner holds and controls | The partner's PA authorization | API orchestration |
| Reimburse-via-payroll export | The employer's salary account via the payroll run | The employer's existing banking | Data export only |

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="Bank file (default)">

We generate a bulk-payment file in the customer's bank format (ICICI CIB, HDFC ENet, Axis PayPro, SBI, Kotak). Finance downloads it from the payout-batch builder, uploads and authorizes it in their own corporate net-banking, and the debit hits their own current account. The PayoutBatch walks `building` → `exported` → `executed` (customer confirms, or we ingest the bank's return file) → `reconciled` against UTRs. Pure file generation is the cleanest possible posture.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payout API (optional)">

A PA-licensed partner of RazorpayX class initiates NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, or UPI payouts; funds debit an account the partner holds and controls, never ours. Each PayoutInstruction maps to one partner payout and the batch walks `building` → `executing` → `completed` or `partially_failed` → `reconciled` on webhooks. Indicative cost ₹1.75 to ₹5 per transaction (vendor pricing, medium confidence). Two contractual musts: recipient KYC, where it applies, sits with the partner; and the contract states in plain terms that we never have dominion over funds.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Payroll export">

Approved amounts export per employee to the payroll system, and the reimbursement rides the salary run. We emit data, not payment instructions, so there is no payment flow on our side at all. This is the right default for companies that already reimburse monthly alongside salary.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

:::danger[Product invariant: funds never touch our account]

Funds never sit in or flow through an account we own or control. Nominal pass-through and pre-funding structures defeat the exemption: an escrow, nodal, pooled, or virtual account in our name counts as handling funds even when a partner bank hosts it, because control, not custody location, is the test. "It only sits there for a second" is still pooling. Codify the invariant in architecture, contracts, and marketing copy, exactly as EnKash ("not a bank, does not hold or claim to hold a banking license") and Open state it.

:::

## What quietly defeats the exemption

The exemption survives only with zero dominion over the money at every step. Each structure below gets pitched somewhere as a convenience; each one re-characterizes the platform.

| Structure | Why it breaks | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pooled or virtual-account pre-funding ("top up with us so payouts run faster") | Customer money lands in an account we control before disbursement: receiving and pooling, the PA trigger; framed as a spendable balance it is a PPI as well | <span class="pill pill--block">Block</span> |
| Nominal pass-through or sweep accounts | Control is the test, not custody location; momentary custody is still pooling | <span class="pill pill--block">Block</span> |
| Marketplace-style collection (collect from companies, settle onward from our pool) | Matches the 2025 PA definition word for word | <span class="pill pill--block">Block</span> |
| Credits that become cashable or transferable | Closed-loop value usable only in-platform is not a payment system; cashable value is a PPI | <span class="pill pill--block">Block</span> |
| Cross-border reimbursement | FEMA regime: purpose codes, AD-bank involvement, a PA-CB licence for the partner (outward cap ₹25 lakh per transaction; analyst, medium confidence) | <span class="pill pill--caution">Deferred</span> |

Cross-border is out of scope for Phase 1 and stays out in Phase 2: [vendor payouts](/phase-2/payouts-and-ap/) remain domestic. Reopening it is a counsel-first decision, not a feature ticket: FEMA is a different statute, and the domestic answer does not carry over.

:::note[The enforcement record reads the same way]

In December 2022 RBI barred Razorpay, Cashfree, Stripe, Paytm, and PayU from onboarding new merchants for their collections (PA) business pending final licences. RazorpayX, the payouts and current-account product, kept onboarding throughout; Razorpay received final PA approval in December 2023. The regulator moved against collections aggregation, not outbound payouts (news reporting, high confidence).

:::

## Collecting money for credits is different

New in v2, and worth stating plainly so nobody conflates the two flows: we do collect money. Companies buy Credit Packs (the ₹499 Top-up through the ₹74,999 Enterprise pack, GST 18% extra) by UPI, netbanking, or card. That is ordinary merchant revenue for our own software, collected through a PA-licensed provider (Razorpay PG class) in exactly the merchant role the framework contemplates: the licensed entity in the middle is regulated so that merchants like us do not need to be. Two properties keep this categorically separate from payment aggregation:

- The money is consideration for software. It settles to our current account as revenue, invoiced with GST, and is never disbursed onward to anyone's employees or vendors. Third-party funds in transit do not exist on our side of the flow.
- Credits are closed-loop metering, not stored value. A `pack_purchase` entry in the CreditEntry ledger buys claim submissions (1 credit = 1 Claim submitted into the workflow); credits are non-refundable, expire on schedule (12-month pack validity), and are never cashable or transferable to third parties. They are a meter reading, not a balance of money.

So when a company at zero balance watches submissions queue in `awaiting_credits` and buys a pack that drains the queue FIFO, that purchase is a SaaS transaction, not a wallet load. Anyone who hears "credits" and thinks "stored value" gets pointed here, then at [credits and metering](/phase-1/credits-and-metering/) and the [pricing ladder](/strategy/pricing/). One adjacent caveat the thesis carries: prepaid-credit revenue recognition (deferred revenue, breakage, GST invoicing) needs CA signoff. That is accounting, not licensing.

## Before go-live

:::caution[Exclusion is not immunity]

The 2025 Directions contain no positive carve-out naming payout-only platforms. Our out-of-scope conclusion is by exclusion from a collections-only definition: the consensus reading of multiple law firms (Saraf and Partners, Trilegal, AZB), but still a reading. Operating an unauthorized payment system under the PSS Act is the severe-end failure mode, so the steps below are gating, not advisory.

:::

<Steps>

1. **Commission a written legal opinion** confirming the bank-file and payout-API architectures sit outside PA scope, citing the 2025 Master Direction's collections-only definition. Done before the first paying customer runs a payout.

2. **Re-verify the live Master Direction.** Its 31 December 2025 application deadline and 28 February 2026 wind-down for non-applicants have passed; the regime is steady-state as of mid-2026. Re-read the PA-CB and third-party payout provisions against the current text, not our research snapshot.

3. **Make the invariant testable.** Architecture review asserts: no service holds credentials that can debit a Kharcha-owned account toward a customer payout; a PayoutBatch can only emit files or call partner APIs; no table anywhere records customer money as a balance we hold.

4. **Put the invariant in contracts and marketing copy**, the EnKash pattern, so sales never improvises a pooled-account convenience that lawyers later have to unwind.

</Steps>
