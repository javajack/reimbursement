Scoping Phase 1 to employees only shrinks the compliance surface to three binding regimes: Income Tax on the employee's side of the money, GST on the purchase underneath the Claim, and record retention. Everything vendor-shaped (TDS, reverse charge, self-invoicing, e-invoice checks) waits for Phase 2. RBI and DPDP are fenced into their own pages. One engineering rule spans all of it: every rate and threshold is effective-dated data, never a constant.

## What binds in Phase 1

The v1 dossier treated reimbursement as one four-regime problem. The employee-only scope splits it: an employee claiming a meal or a cab is not a vendor, so withholding and reverse charge never arise. What remains:

| Regime | What it means for us | Product mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Income Tax (employee side) | Pure cost-to-cost reimbursement against a bill is not salary income. The taxable shapes are allowances and perquisites: unspent per-diem (Sec 10(14), Rule 2BB), car and fuel support (Rule 3 values), gifts above ₹5,000 per FY. | Informational payroll-feed flag on the unspent per-diem balance; PER_DIEM_CAP; Rule 3 values as category reference data; gift-cap `warn`. Never blocks payment. |
| GST (ITC eligibility) | The employee is not a supplier; GST rides on the merchant invoice underneath the Claim. Sec 17(5) blocks ITC on most T&E categories; ordinary business travel stays creditable. We never compute returns in Phase 1. | GSTIN_FORMAT validation (Luhn-mod-36); CGST/SGST/IGST split at extraction; ITC_ELIGIBLE tag per LineItem, informational for finance exports. |
| Record retention | Three clocks run concurrently: Companies Act 8 FY (the binding longest), GST 72 months, Income Tax 6 years from AY end. | 8 FY default retention on Receipts and AuditEvents; litigation hold freezes deletion during proceedings. |

<p class="source-note">Source: CGST Sec 17(5), Income Tax Secs 10(14)/37 and Rules 2BB/3, Companies Act Sec 128, CGST Sec 36 (govt/primary corroborated, high confidence; see provenance below).</p>

A claim can be perfectly payable yet carry consequences in the company's GST credit and the employee's payroll; Phase 1 models both as informational overlays on the LineItem, never as payment blockers.

The fences: RBI requires no PA or PPI authorization for our posture (bank-file payouts, funds never in our accounts; confirmed, high), with a written legal opinion gating go-live; see [Money Movement](/compliance/money-movement/). Under DPDP 2023 we are a data processor (Rules notified 14 Nov 2025, full obligations around May 2027, ₹250 crore ceiling, Sec 8(5)); see [Data Protection](/compliance/data-protection/).

## Income Tax: the employee's side of the money

When an employee spends on a business purpose and hands over the bill, reimbursing exactly that amount creates no income in their hands. Taxability appears where the flow stops being cost-to-cost: allowances paid regardless of spend, perquisites with fixed values, gifts. The company-side test (Sec 37(1), wholly and exclusively for business) is why challans, fines, and personal spend ship as default non-reimbursable categories in [Policy and Rules](/phase-1/onboarding/policy-and-rules/).

| Item | Rule | Treatment | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per-diem / daily allowance | Sec 10(14)(i), Rule 2BB | Exempt only to the extent actually spent on official duty; the unspent balance is taxable salary | <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> |
| Car running and maintenance | Rule 3 | Perquisite of ₹1,800/month (engine up to 1.6 litres) or ₹2,400/month (above), plus ₹900/month driver; nil if wholly official with logbook and employer certificate | <span class="pill pill--med">Medium</span> |
| Fuel as a flat allowance | Practitioner interpretation | A ~₹7,000/month exempt cap circulates; no clean statutory limit; model against actual bills under Rule 3 | <span class="pill pill--low">Low</span> |
| Gifts and vouchers | Rule 3(7)(iv) | Exempt up to ₹5,000 aggregate per FY; the excess is taxable; cash gifts fully taxable | <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> |

<p class="source-note">Source: Rule 2BB and Rule 3 (incometaxindia.gov.in), TaxGuru per-diem analysis. Rule 3 rupee values are practitioner-corroborated (medium) with a draft 2026 car-perquisite revision reportedly in flight; the fuel cap is a low-confidence practitioner reading.</p>

Phase 1 computes nobody's tax. PER_DIEM_CAP (city-tier × grade) governs what is claimable; the tax overlay is a separate informational flag, microcopy like `Unspent per-diem ₹2,500: potentially taxable, review in payroll`, carried into the reimburse-via-payroll export. Of the six rule actions (`allow`, `flag`, `warn`, `cap`, `block`, `route`), the payroll tax overlays (unspent per-diem, gift cap) use only `flag` and `warn`; ITC_ELIGIBLE is `allow` with an informational tag: paying is a policy decision, taxability is information.

## GST: the employee is not the supplier

A reimbursement to an employee is not a supply by the employee to the company; no GST arises on the reimbursement leg. The GST that matters was charged by the merchant on the underlying purchase, and finance's only question is whether that tax is claimable as input credit. CGST Sec 17(5) answers, mostly with no for T&E.

We tag, we never file: no return computation, no GSTR-2B reconciliation in-product (v1 imagined one; v2 cuts it). ITC_ELIGIBLE runs as an `allow`-verb informational rule: it tags eligible vs blocked, it never gates; an employee's meal stays payable while its credit is blocked. The tag flows into finance exports; finance reconciles in their own GST workflow. Precision still matters because wrongly availed and utilised credit attracts 18% per annum interest under Sec 50(3) as amended by Finance Act 2022 (older sources still quote 24%; this rate sits on the CA go-live verification list), and that exposure is the customer's. The clause map is <span class="pill pill--high">High</span> confidence, verified against the CBIC repository text.

| Clause | Blocks ITC on | Exception path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17(5)(a) | Motor vehicles for transport of persons, approved seating capacity not more than 13 including driver | Further supply (resale), passenger transport, driving training |
| 17(5)(ab) | Insurance, servicing, repair and maintenance of those vehicles | Tracks clause (a): available where the vehicle itself escapes the block |
| 17(5)(b)(i) | Food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatment, health services, leasing/renting/hiring of motor vehicles (rent-a-cab), life and health insurance | Same-category outward taxable supply or taxable composite/mixed supply; obligatory-under-law also applies |
| 17(5)(b)(ii) | Membership of a club, health and fitness centre | Obligatory-under-law proviso |
| 17(5)(b)(iii) | Travel benefits to employees on vacation (leave or home travel concession) | Obligatory-under-law proviso |

<p class="source-note">Source: CBIC tax repository verbatim text for clause (b), IndianKanoon for clause (a), ClearTax clause-by-clause guide (primary corroborated, high confidence).</p>

The obligatory-under-law proviso covers all of clause (b) effective 1 February 2019 (CGST Amendment Act 2018), the same amendment that introduced the 13-seat wording. Both exception paths are company-profile justification flags defaulting to false; ITC stays tagged blocked unless explicitly justified.

:::caution[The nuance that wrecks naive rule packs]
"Travel benefits extended to employees on vacation" means leave or home travel concession (LTC/HTC), nothing more. Ordinary business travel, air or rail for actual work, is generally creditable and not blocked by Sec 17(5). Blanket-blocking every travel category strips legitimate credit; we tag business travel eligible by default.
:::

What the tag looks like on the wire, for a ₹1,050 intrastate restaurant bill:

```json
{
  "id": "li_01J9ZD3M8Q2WJX5TVE6RB0KAGN",
  "claim_id": "clm_01J9ZD2H7P4N8XKQW3CYB5DMTF",
  "category": "meals",
  "merchant_gstin": "29ABCDE1234F1Z5",
  "amount_paise": 105000,
  "currency": "INR",
  "tax_split": { "cgst_paise": 2500, "sgst_paise": 2500, "igst_paise": 0 },
  "itc_tag": {
    "status": "blocked",
    "clause": "cgst_17_5_b_i",
    "informational": true,
    "rule": "ITC_ELIGIBLE",
    "rule_version": 3
  }
}
```

:::tip[Informational does not mean unimportant]
GST-grade capture (employer GSTIN, per-line CGST/SGST/IGST split) is rarely automated by incumbents; vendor marketing claims up to 60% of T&E ITC goes unclaimed (directional, not audited). We already itemise at extraction, so the Sec 17(5) tag costs nothing extra and gives finance a rupee number to care about.
:::

## Retention: the longest clock wins

| Law | Period | Anchor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Companies Act 2013, Sec 128 | 8 financial years | Preceding the current year; longer if an investigation is ordered |
| CGST Act, Sec 36 | 72 months | From the annual-return due date; plus one year after final disposal where proceedings are pending |
| Income Tax | 6 years | From the end of the relevant assessment year |

<p class="source-note">Source: Companies Act Sec 128 via CAIRR, CGST Sec 36 via AUBSP (govt/primary corroborated, high confidence).</p>

Build to 8 FY on everything: Receipt images, line items, AuditEvents, approval trails. A litigation hold freezes deletion for anything under appeal, investigation, or proceeding; both the GST extension and the Companies Act investigation power assume one. Mechanics live in [Audit and Retention](/compliance/audit-and-retention/).

## What arrives with Phase 2

The moment the payee is a vendor rather than an employee, the company becomes a withholder and sometimes the taxpayer. None of it leaks into Phase 1 code. The TDS surface, quoted mainly as evidence of churn:

| Section | Covers | Rate | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 194C | Contractors | 1% individual/HUF, 2% others | ₹30,000 single contract; ₹1,00,000 aggregate per FY |
| 194J | Professional / technical services | 10% professional, 2% technical | ₹50,000 from FY 2025-26 (was ₹30,000) |
| 194H | Commission / brokerage | 2% from 1 Oct 2024 (was 5%) | ₹20,000 per FY from 1 Apr 2025 (was ₹15,000) |
| Any of the above, no PAN | Missing PAN | 20% | n/a |

<p class="source-note">Source: incometaxindia.gov.in TDS rates, ClearTax chart, Bajaj Finserv 194H note (govt/primary corroborated, high; re-confirm against the Finance Act for the relevant FY).</p>

Beyond TDS: reverse charge (RCM) on notified services (GTA, advocate legal services, rent-a-cab where the supplier elects; aggregator rides fall under Sec 9(5), the platform pays), self-invoicing for RCM purchases with offsetting ITC where Sec 17(5) does not block, and e-invoice checks (IRN/QR presence where e-invoicing applies to the supplier). RCM is notification-specific, never blanket-applied. All of it lands as vendor-scoped Rule types in [Vendor Tax Engine](/phase-2/vendor-tax-engine/).

## Version-dating: rates are data, not constants

Every rate, threshold, and clause reference resolves by the financial year of the expense date, from an effective-dated table, never from a constant in code. The 2024-2025 churn alone would have silently broken a constants file three times: 194H's rate on 1 October 2024, 194H's threshold on 1 April 2025, 194J's threshold at FY 2025-26.

```json
{
  "key": "tds_rate_194h",
  "scope": "phase_2",
  "value_bps": 200,
  "effective_from": "2024-10-01",
  "effective_to": null,
  "supersedes": { "value_bps": 500, "effective_to": "2024-09-30" },
  "provenance": "secondary_corroborated"
}
```

The Rule contract already carries this: every Rule has `effective_from`, `effective_to`, and `version`; every firing writes an AuditEvent with rule id, version, and input snapshot, so a 2028 assessment can reconstruct which value applied to a 2026 Claim. Schema in [Rule Schema](/spec/rule-schema/).

The review cadence is a runbook, not a habit:

<Steps>

1. **Watch four triggers.** Union Budget (1 February), Finance Act assent, GST Council notifications, CBDT circulars.

2. **Stage, never edit.** Diff the rates pack against the new text; stage new rows with `effective_from` set to the statutory date. Mid-year dates are real (see 194H); never assume 1 April.

3. **CA signoff.** A chartered accountant approves the staged pack before activation.

4. **Activate and audit.** Prior rows get `effective_to`; the change writes an AuditEvent; historical rows are never edited, so past claims re-resolve identically forever.

</Steps>
