Kharcha holds exactly the data the DPDP Act 2023 cares about: employee identities, payout bank accounts, salary-adjacent spend histories. The posture is narrow: Data Processor for customer employee data, Data Fiduciary only for our own staff and for customer billing contacts. The ₹250 crore headline is a statutory ceiling, not an invoice, and full obligations land around May 2027, under a year out. The near-term cost of DPDP is buyer security questionnaires, not fines; this page sets the consent, retention, and breach model that answers both. <span class="pill pill--build">Conditional Build</span>

## Roles: processor first, fiduciary twice

The role split decides who carries the duties. [The regulatory map](/compliance/regulatory-map/) cleared the money-movement gate; this is the data gate.

| Data set | Our role | Who carries the fiduciary duties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer employees' personal data (identity, bank details, claims, receipts) | Data Processor | The customer company; we process on documented instructions |
| Customer billing contacts (name, email, GSTIN, credit-pack purchases) | Data Fiduciary | Us; we collect this for our own billing purpose |
| Our own staff's personal data | Data Fiduciary | Us, directly |

Two precision points the marketing version of this law gets wrong, both confirmed at high confidence in our fact-check:

- DPDP has no GDPR-style sensitive-data tier. PAN, bank, and expense data are all just "personal data": no higher bar for financial fields, but the baseline duties apply to everything we hold.
- DPDP does not govern Aadhaar; the Aadhaar Act 2016 and UIDAI do. Phase 1 collects no Aadhaar at all. PAN is an optional employee field only; product identity is work email plus employee ID. Vendor KYC (PAN, GSTIN, Udyam) is a Phase 2 concern.

:::note[Why the split matters commercially]

As a processor we reduce direct fiduciary exposure, but the buyer's own DPDP risk becomes their reason to interrogate our security. The questionnaire is the deal gate, not a hypothetical fine on us.

:::

## Ceilings, not invoices: the penalty schedule and the clock

The Schedule under Sec 33 sets maxima per violation type; the Data Protection Board sets actual amounts on nature, gravity, duration, volume, and harm, and violations can stack. Read it as a ranked exposure map, not a price list.

| Violation (DPDP Schedule) | Maximum penalty | Relevance to Kharcha |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Failure of reasonable security safeguards, Sec 8(5) | Up to ₹250 crore | The ceiling; the tier tied to a breach of employee bank or identity data |
| Failure to notify a personal data breach, Sec 8(6) | Up to ₹200 crore | Makes the 72-hour runbook below a compliance artefact |
| Children's-data obligations, Sec 9 | Up to ₹200 crore | Low relevance; workforce data |
| Significant Data Fiduciary obligations, Sec 10 | Up to ₹150 crore | Only if designated an SDF, unlikely at SMB scale |
| Breach of any other provision (residual) | Up to ₹50 crore | Catch-all |

<p class="source-note">Source: DPDP Act 2023 Schedule, Sec 33 (primary, high confidence). Statutory maxima set case by case by the Board, not typical fines.</p>

The law is real but staggered, and building against the endpoint now is the cheap path:

| Milestone | Date | Status (Jun 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DPDP Rules 2025 notified in the Gazette | 14 Nov 2025 | Done; Board and definitions live |
| Consent-manager registration window (~12 months) | ~Nov 2026, est. | Phasing in |
| Full data-fiduciary obligations (notice, consent, rights, breach, retention, security) | ~May 2027 | Roughly 11 months of runway |

<p class="source-note">Source: DPDP Rules 2025 notification (govt, high); EY and India Briefing implementation analyses (analyst, medium). Treat ~May 2027 as approximate.</p>

:::caution[Do not launder the ceiling into a likely fine]

Enforcement is nascent: the Board became operational in late 2025 and SMB-scale penalties are untested. The realistic near-term cost is questionnaire friction and stalled deals. Quote the number as a ceiling, every time.

:::

## The data inventory

Everything below is personal data under DPDP. The inventory is deliberately small: no Aadhaar, no card numbers, no salary figures, bank details only where payouts need them.

| Data | Entities | Principal | Handling notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Employee identity: name, work email, phone, employee ID, department, grade | User, Membership, Department, Grade | Customer employee | PAN optional only; never Aadhaar; tenant-scoped by row-level security |
| Payout bank details | BeneficiaryAccount | Customer employee | Account number encrypted at rest; UI, exports, and logs show the last 4 digits only; verification-gated before the first payout run |
| Receipt images | Receipt | Customer employee, plus incidental third parties | A medical bill names a patient; a hotel folio carries a phone number. Every Receipt is personal data regardless of category |
| Claims and line items | Claim, LineItem | Customer employee | Salary-adjacent spend history; amounts as `amount_paise`; retained 8 financial years |
| Approver decisions | ApprovalStep, AuditEvent | Customer approver | Actor, role at decision time, channel (web, web_push, email_link; native_push once native apps ship), IP and device; append-only |
| Billing and credits | CreditEntry, billing profile | Billing contact (we are fiduciary) | Name, email, GSTIN, purchase history for invoicing |

Receipts live in Mumbai-region (ap-south-1) object storage behind signed URLs; the database is PostgreSQL with per-tenant row-level security. The control-by-control treatment (encryption, key management, access tiers) is in [the security spec](/spec/security-and-compliance/).

## Consent: captured at onboarding, scoped to reimbursement

DPDP consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional, and affirmative. We capture it once, at the natural moment, never buried in terms.

| Consent point | Where it happens | What we record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Itemised notice plus consent | First login from the 7-day invite magic link, before any claim is drafted (see [company onboarding](/phase-1/onboarding/company-onboarding/)) | Notice version, timestamp, language shown, the affirmative action, as an AuditEvent |
| Purpose limitation | Product behaviour and terms | Data used to process reimbursements: capture, approvals, payout files, statutory reports. Never advertising; receipts never train models without explicit opt-in |
| Language requirement | Notice rendering | The Act requires the notice in English or an Eighth Schedule language; our vernacular-first UI (Hindi plus 2-3 state languages at launch) makes this cheap to honour |
| Withdrawal | Profile settings, as easy as granting | Stops further processing; statutory retention of financial records stands, stated plainly in the notice |

### Channel consent: email and web push

Phase 1 has exactly three notification channels, all employment-context and transactional: `in_app` (a notifications inbox in the PWA/web Console), `email` (transactional, the universal guaranteed channel), and `web_push` (Web Push over VAPID to an installed PWA, best-effort on iOS). The onboarding notice covers all three; no separate channel opt-in is needed because the copy is strictly transactional reimbursement workflow, scoped to the same reimbursement purpose as the rest of the product.

Approver email actions use signed single-use magic-link buttons inside that transactional email channel. Web push needs the browser's own subscription permission, which the user grants at the OS/browser layer when they install the PWA. The future native mobile apps add `native_push` (FCM/APNs) over the API; that is the only additional channel on the roadmap. The email provider and the web-push service are named sub-processors (see the processor contract below); mechanics live in [the approver experience](/phase-1/web/approver-experience/).

## Rights handling

We build the tooling; the customer, as fiduciary, fields the requests. Every right ships as an admin-usable feature, not a support ticket.

| Right | How we serve it |
| --- | --- |
| Access (per-principal access export; the GDPR world calls this a DSAR) | Admin-triggered per-principal export: profile, Claims, LineItems, Receipt images, decision history, in machine-readable form |
| Correction | Self-serve profile edits; BeneficiaryAccount changes re-trigger verification before the next payout run (penny-drop ~₹3 per check, Setu pricing, high confidence) |
| Erasure | Anonymisation, not deletion: identifiers stripped, financial skeleton retained (see below) |
| Grievance | Published grievance contact with an acknowledgement SLA, surfaced in the notice and the app footer; escalation routes to the customer fiduciary |

:::danger[Erasure vs the 8 FY retention rule: statute wins, anonymisation reconciles]

DPDP says erase when the purpose ends. Companies Act Sec 128 says keep books 8 financial years, the longest binding window (GST 72 months and Income Tax 6 years from AY end sit inside it). The resolution: on a valid erasure request we anonymise the principal (name, contact, bank identifiers replaced with irreversible tokens) and retain the financial record (Claim, LineItem, amounts, GST fields, approval trail) to statute. We never delete financial records to satisfy an erasure request. Mechanics in [audit and retention](/compliance/audit-and-retention/).

:::

## The breach runbook

The Rules are broader than GDPR: no risk-based de-minimis threshold, so essentially every qualifying incident notifies affected individuals, not just the Board.

<Steps>

1. **Contain and preserve.** Isolate the vector, freeze AuditEvent and access logs, start the clock.

2. **Scope.** Which tenants, which principals, which data classes (identity, bank details, receipts).

3. **Intimate the Data Protection Board without delay.** The first notice precedes the full report.

4. **File the detailed report within 72 hours.** Nature, scope, remediation, point of contact.

5. **Notify affected individuals without delay,** in plain language, routed via the customer where the contract assigns it.

6. **Notify affected customers** so they can run their own fiduciary clocks; publish the internal root cause.

</Steps>

Pager ownership, drill cadence, and the evidence pack template live in [ops runbooks](/phase-1/ops-runbooks/).

## The processor contract customers will ask for

Mid-market buyers send a data processing agreement before signature. Pre-write it.

| Clause | What we commit |
| --- | --- |
| Documented instructions | Processing limited to the reimbursement purpose; no secondary use |
| Sub-processors | Named list (cloud, OCR provider, transactional email provider, web-push service) with advance notice of changes |
| Security safeguards | Encryption in transit and at rest, tenant isolation, access control, append-only audit logs |
| Breach notice to customer | Within a contractual window tighter than 72 hours, so the customer can meet their own Board clock |
| Rights assistance | Per-principal access export (DSAR), correction, and anonymisation tooling at no extra charge |
| Audit and evidence | Certifications plus completed questionnaires in lieu of on-site audits at SMB tier |
| Return and deletion | Full export on termination, deletion minus the statutory carve-out, with a deletion certificate |
| Residency | Mumbai region (ap-south-1) primary; cross-border transfers only per Rule 15's negative-list model, permissive today unless a country is restricted |

## The buyer-trust sequence

Certifications are how we pass the IT/Security gate without a bespoke months-long audit per deal. The order matters more than the spend.

| Step | Indicative cost and timeline | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 27001 | ~₹5 lakh year one plus ~₹60,000/yr surveillance (analyst/vendor, medium); 12-16 weeks | Before the first enterprise pitch; the India-and-EU preferred signal |
| VAPT | Periodic: annual plus major releases | From the first paying customer; the direct evidence of Sec 8(5) safeguards |
| SOC 2 Type II | ₹6-14 lakh; 12-plus weeks (vendor, medium) | Only when a US-headquartered buyer demands it; never pre-spend |

<p class="source-note">Source: Codesecure and TCSA India SaaS compliance-cost analyses (analyst/vendor, medium confidence). Get current quotes before budgeting.</p>

:::tip[Sequencing is the whole point]

ISO 27001 plus DPDP-as-a-marketed-feature clears most Indian enterprise and EU questionnaires for about ₹5 lakh. SOC 2 earns its ₹6-14 lakh only when a US-HQ deal funds it; buying it first is a classic cash-burn mistake.

:::

Carry the honest caveat into every sales conversation: enforcement is young, the Board's posture toward SMB-scale processors is unwritten, and the DPDP tax we actually pay this year is measured in questionnaire hours. Build the artefacts on this page once, answer fast forever.
